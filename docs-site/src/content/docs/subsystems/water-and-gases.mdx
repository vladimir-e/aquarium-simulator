## What it models

Oxygen and CO₂ are stored as concentrations and relax toward equilibrium at a
rate flow and aeration set. Between those relaxations sits the tick's real
budget: every aerobic process in the tank — fish, plants at night, rot, both
nitrifier guilds — takes its oxygen out of the same stock, and each one's rate
saturates against what is left, but for the maintenance a fish owes whatever
the water holds. Carbon carries it: what oxidises organic matter breathes its
carbon out as CO₂ and draws the oxygen that carbon's formula takes. Carbonate
hardness (KH) is the third stock and general hardness (GH) the fourth, and pH is
not a stock at all: it is read off CO₂ against KH wherever something needs it.

## How it works

### Storage

Gases are stored as **concentration** in mg/L, and nitrogen compounds as
**mass** in mg. That asymmetry is deliberate and it decides who divides: an
organism system answers a biological question and returns a mass, so plants,
fish and rot each divide their gas mass by standing water before pushing the
effect. It is why the same planting moves a nano further than it moves a 300 L.

Alkalinity and general hardness are stored as **mass** too, both in mg of
CaCO₃, and read as dKH and dGH: a German degree is `10` mg/L of CaO, so
`10 × MW_CaCO₃ / MW_CaO` mg/L of CaCO₃ for either.

Bounds clamp oxygen to `0`–`20` mg/L, CO₂ to `0`–`100` and KH and GH to no
less than zero.

### Gas exchange

```
saturation = 10.08 − 0.17 × (temperature − 15)      mg/L, floored at 4
turnovers  = flow / capacity
flowFactor = max(minFlowFactor, min(1, turnovers / optimalFlowTurnover))
delta      = baseExchangeRate × aerationFactor × flowFactor × (target − current)
```

Oxygen's target is that saturation; CO₂'s is a flat atmospheric `4.0` mg/L.
Aeration multiplies the rate by 3, and for CO₂ by a further 1.5 — which is the
mechanism behind the oldest trade-off in a planted tank, since an air stone
buys oxygen by stripping the carbon.

| Temperature | O₂ saturation |
|---|---|
| 18 °C | `9.57` mg/L |
| 22 °C | `8.89` mg/L |
| 25 °C | `8.38` mg/L |
| 28 °C | `7.87` mg/L |
| 30 °C | `7.53` mg/L |

| 100 L setup | Flow factor | Effective rate | Half-life of a gas deficit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing running | `0.10` — the diffusion floor | 2.5 %/h | 27 h |
| Canister, no aeration | `0.80` | 20 %/h | 3.1 h |
| Sponge filter, which aerates | `0.30` × 3 | 22.5 %/h | 2.7 h |
| Canister + powerhead | `1.00` | 25 %/h | 2.4 h |
| Canister + powerhead + air pump | `1.00` × 3 | 75 %/h | 0.5 h |

A sponge filter beats a bare canister on gas exchange in the same tank while
moving well under half the water, because it aerates and the canister does not.
Aeration, not flow, is the dominant lever.

### Injected CO₂

The injector meters a mass (its figure is on [Equipment](/subsystems/equipment/));
off-gassing is the CO₂ half of the exchange above. The tank settles where the two
balance, less what the plants fix:

```
steady CO₂ ≈ atmospheric + (injection − plant uptake) / (volume × baseExchangeRate × flowFactor)
```

The high-tech scenario — 40 gal, canister, 2 bps from an hour before the lights
— climbs from ~5 mg/L at dawn to ~29 by lights-off, and pH swings ~0.75 over the
day across the KH its soil has stripped.

### The tick's carbon–oxygen budget

Every aerobic draw but an organism's maintenance multiplies its rate by
`O₂ / (K + O₂)` and quotes its own `K`; maintenance burns reserve regardless,
a fish's or a flora's oxygen pace scaling only what it takes in and mobilises.
Both gases are read off the carbon each process moves, so each is one reaction
rather than two coefficients.

| Process | Oxygen | Carbon | `K` (mg/L) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | + fixed CO₂ × `0.727` | − each fixer's Monod draw on the dissolved pool | `0.50`, on what it fixes ([Plants](/subsystems/plants/#photosynthesis)) |
| Spores | + fixed CO₂ × `0.727` | − the CO₂ they are built of, on their bloom's Monod beside every fixer | — |
| Flora respiration | − CO₂ released × `0.727` | + | `0.50`, on mobilisation and repair |
| Fish metabolism | − `1.456` mg per mg of reserve burned; − `2.664` per mg of carbon in a meal's excess | + `3.664` per mg of carbon either releases | `1.00`, on intake and mobilisation only ([Livestock](/subsystems/livestock/#the-ledger)) |
| Aerobic rot | − `2.664` per mg of carbon oxidised | + `3.664` per mg of carbon | `0.20` |
| Anaerobic rot | — | + `3.664` per mg of carbon | slides in as the aerobic path runs short |
| Ammonia oxidation | − `2.819` mg per mg NH₃ | — | `0.30` |
| Nitrite oxidation | − `0.348` mg per mg NO₂⁻ | — | `1.10` |
| Aeration bubbles | + `0.05` mg/L/hr, capped by the gap to saturation | — | — |
| Gas exchange | ± toward saturation | ± toward atmospheric | — |

| `K` | Rate left at 8.38 mg/L | Rate left at 2.0 mg/L |
|---|---|---|
| `0.20` — rot | 97.7 % | 90.9 % |
| `0.30` — AOB | 96.5 % | 87.0 % |
| `1.10` — NOB | 88.4 % | 64.5 % |

Three consequences, and all three are the point. **Demand falls with supply** —
but for maintenance, a tank cannot draw oxygen it does not have, because the
draw shrinks as the stock does. **Rot slows rather than stops** — as the
oxygen runs out it slides to its anaerobic pace, a fifth of the aerobic by
default, drawing none and still breathing its carbon out as CO₂. **The guild
that needs the most air suffers first** — NOB carry nearly four times AOB's constant, which is where
standing nitrite in an under-aerated tank comes from. Fish and flora are the
draws that never stop: their oxygen pace, read against saturated water, starves
them of intake and growth as the water empties, while their maintenance burns
whatever the oxygen.

Every base rate multiplied by that factor is a Monod *maximum*, reached only at
infinite oxygen, so what a tank at air saturation reproduces is the fourth
column above rather than the constant itself.

### Ordering within the tick

Environment and equipment settle first, so CO₂ injection is in the water before
plants run. Plants, the blooms and their spores fix and respire carbon in one
pass, drawing on one CO₂ stock together, each on its own Monod read at the CO₂
the hour leaves — one solve, so no fixer gets first pick and together they never
take the last milligram. Then livestock apply theirs. Gas exchange, rot and the
nitrogen cycle run last on **one oxygen solve**: every aerobic process in the
tier runs on the oxygen there is, gas exchange included — each reads its Monod
at the level all their draws and the air leave the water at as the hour ends.

### KH and pH

```
dKH = kh / water / CACO3_PER_DEGREE
pH  = 7 − log10((co2 + 0.1) / (3 × (dKH + 0.1)))
```

pH follows CO₂ and KH the moment either moves: a unit per tenfold change in
their ratio. KH is the buffer — the same CO₂ plume swings a soft tank much
further than a hard one — and without it pH falls toward ~5.9 rather than
without limit.

| CO₂ | KH 0 | KH 1 | KH 4 | KH 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `4` mg/L — atmospheric | `5.86` | `6.91` | `7.48` | `7.77` |
| `15` mg/L | `5.30` | `6.34` | `6.91` | `7.21` |
| `30` mg/L — injected | `5.00` | `6.04` | `6.61` | `6.91` |

What moves KH:

| Flow | Direction | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Tap water | + | `tapKh` per litre added — a fill, a water change, a top-off or the ATO |
| Evaporation | concentrates | the mass stays, the water goes |
| Nitrogen | − or + | an equivalent, `CACO3_PER_EQUIVALENT` = `MW_CaCO3 / 2`, per proton — below |
| Calcite rock | + | `calciteDissolutionRate` per rock at pH 7, ×10 per pH unit lower |
| Driftwood | − | `tanninLeachRate` of the piece's remaining tannins per hour |
| Aqua soil | − | `aquaSoilKhUptake` of the tank's KH per hour, scaled by the share of the bed's buffer left |

Nitrogen moves KH by its charge: KH gains an equivalent for each proton a
process takes from the water and loses one for each it releases.

| Process | Protons taken per nitrogen |
|---|---|
| Ammonia minted from organic nitrogen — at the gills, or out of a rotting pool: NH₃ + H⁺ → NH₄⁺ | `+1` |
| Nitrification, NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ ([Nitrogen cycle](/subsystems/nitrogen-cycle/)) | `−2` |
| Plants and the blooms taking ammonia | `−1` |
| Plants and the blooms taking nitrate, from the water or the bed | `+1` |

While KH lasts, these processes move it exactly with the charge on the
inorganic nitrogen in the water and the bed, so organic nitrogen carried to
nitrate nets one equivalent spent, whatever flora it passes through on the way. Every other flow here — and a fertiliser dose or a
water change — moves KH and that charge independently.

Four hobby behaviours fall out. A heavily stocked tank left without water
changes drains its KH and its pH sags — old tank syndrome. A lightly stocked
one topped off with hard tap water creeps the other way. A calcite scape
settles where dissolution, slowing as pH climbs, balances what the tank spends.
And a fresh soil tank starts acidic and drifts toward tap chemistry over the
months its buffer takes to run down — the reserves themselves are on
[Equipment](/subsystems/equipment/). Plants slow the first: a planting that
keeps pace with its stocking holds its KH.

### GH

GH is calcium and magnesium, the hardness species tolerate and plants build
with. It has no chemistry of its own in the tick — the tolerance bands and
plant uptake read it — and it moves with KH wherever the two share a cause:

| Flow | Direction | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Tap water | + | `tapGh` per litre added, wherever tap KH arrives |
| Evaporation | concentrates | the mass stays, the water goes |
| Calcite rock | + | the same mg as its KH — one flow, written to both |
| Aqua soil | − | the same mg as its KH uptake, never more than the water holds of either |
| Plants | − | a fixed share of the nutrients they draw, on [Plants](/subsystems/plants/) |

Driftwood and nitrification spend carbonate, not calcium, so a tank that drains
its KH keeps its GH. A cycled soil tank sits as far below the tap in GH as the
soil took KH, and its KH lower still by the nitrate its month made.

### Stocks and rates

| Stock | Fills it | Drains it | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `oxygen` | Gas exchange toward saturation; aeration bubbles; photosynthesis and spores | Fish, flora respiration, aerobic rot, both nitrifier guilds | mg/L |
| `co2` | Injection; fish, flora respiration, rot | Off-gassing toward atmospheric; photosynthesis and spores | mg/L |
| `kh` | Tap water; calcite; ammonia minted from organic nitrogen; nitrate the flora take | Nitrification; ammonia the flora take; driftwood; aqua soil; the old water a change removes | mg CaCO₃ |
| `gh` | Tap water; calcite | Aqua soil; plants; the old water a change removes | mg CaCO₃ |
