## What it models

Each plant is one unit of its growth form — a carpet patch, a sword, a rhizome
clump — with a size, a condition and a bank, on the
[vitality](/concepts/vitality/) model. It reads light at its own height, under
whatever crowns stand above it: that light drives everything it earns, and the
day of it decides whether it starves. Income at full condition banks; the bank
heals the plant, grows it toward a full unit and then buys an offshoot of its
family. Low condition sheds tissue.

## How it works

### The species roster

Five species, each of one [growth form](#growth-forms), in three tables: how
each grows, the light it lives on, and the water it tolerates.

| Species | Growth rate | CO₂ need | Nutrient demand | Hardiness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anubias | 0.3 | low | low | 0.75 |
| Java Fern | 0.5 | low | low | 0.7 |
| Amazon Sword | 1.0 | medium | medium | 0.5 |
| Dwarf Hairgrass | 1.5 | high | high | 0.3 |
| Monte Carlo | 1.8 | high | high | 0.3 |

Anubias and java fern attach to the scape; the sword and the carpets root in
the bed.

| Species | Tolerable PAR | Saturates at, `Ik` | Starves under |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anubias | 8–70 | 16 | 0.23 |
| Java Fern | 10–90 | 20 | 0.29 |
| Amazon Sword | 20–120 | 40 | 0.58 |
| Dwarf Hairgrass | 25–200 | 50 | 0.72 |
| Monte Carlo | 30–200 | 60 | 0.86 |

PAR is in µmol/m²/s and the starvation edge is a day's light, in mol/m²/d. Both
derive from the band's low end — [saturation](/subsystems/light/#saturation) and
[the day's light](/subsystems/light/#the-days-light) on Light.

| Species | Temperature | pH | GH |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anubias | 18–30 °C | 5.0–8.0 | 1–20 dGH |
| Java Fern | 18–30 °C | 5.0–8.0 | 1–20 dGH |
| Amazon Sword | 20–28 °C | 5.5–7.8 | 2–20 dGH |
| Dwarf Hairgrass | 20–28 °C | 5.0–7.8 | 1–18 dGH |
| Monte Carlo | 20–28 °C | 5.0–7.8 | 1–15 dGH |

pH and GH bands are the generous published ranges, wide enough for aqua soil under CO₂ and hard tap alike. Past an edge the damage grows linearly with the distance, per °C, pH unit or dGH (`temperatureStressSeverity`, `phStressSeverity`, `ghStressSeverity`). pH is already a log scale, so linear in pH is log in acidity. Temperature and pH also earn inside their bands; GH is damage alone.

### Growth forms

A plant's `size` is how full one unit of its growth form is, in % — 100 is a
grown patch, specimen or clump. The form fixes what that means.

| Form | Carpet | Rosette | Attached |
|---|---|---|---|
| Species | Dwarf Hairgrass, Monte Carlo | Amazon Sword | Anubias, Java Fern |
| One unit is | a patch | a specimen | a rhizome clump |
| Full height | 5 cm | 40 cm | 20 cm |
| Height grows as | full at any size — it spreads across | `size^⅓` | `size^⅓` |
| Footprint | 100 cm² | 700 cm² | 200 cm² |
| Leaf area index | 2.5 | 2.0 | 2.5 |
| Rate units at full | 0.5 | 2.8 | 1.0 |
| Offshoot | sends a runner | throws a plantlet | branches at the rhizome |
| Root share `r` | 0.5 — half from each | 0.7 — feeds mostly from the bed | 0 — feeds from the water |

```
height    = min(depth, fullHeight × (size / 100)^exponent)
leafArea  = leafAreaIndex × footprint × size / 100      cm²
rateUnits = leafArea / 500
```

Size says how full the unit is: it drives growth's taper, the height and the
self-shading. Leaf area says how much plant there is: it drives every flux the
tank sees, in rate units of 500 cm² of leaf. No crown stands taller than the
water.

### Photosynthesis

```
// PAR_i at the plant's mean leaf; K_i by the species' CO₂ need
lightResponse_i = tanh(PAR_i / Ik_i)
carbon_i        = rateUnits_i × lightResponse_i × sufficiency_i
K               = Σ carbon_i × K_i / Σ carbon_i
co2 drawn       = monodUptake(CO₂, Σ carbon_i × basePhotosynthesisRate × co2PerRateUnit, K)
```

Carbon saturates like every other limit in the engine. Species differ in how
much carbon they need to run near their ceiling, not in a floor that kills:

| CO₂ need | On 4 mg/L | On 25 mg/L |
|---|---|---|
| low | 73 % | 94 % |
| medium | 57 % | 89 % |
| high | 33 % | 76 % |

So a carpet in an uninjected tank grows slowly on a thin carbon income, and a
sword or an epiphyte hardly notices the injector.

Photosynthesis moves the gases and nothing else. The carbon curve is applied
once, as a draw: the planting takes CO₂ on an end-of-tick Monod at the
carbon-weighted mean of its CO₂ half-saturations, so a dense planting slows
its own draw and never takes the water's last milligram. The drive is summed
across the planting and multiplied by `basePhotosynthesisRate` to give the
tank's rate in rate units, where one rate unit is an hour of 500 cm² of leaf at
saturating carbon and light.

It never adds size: growth flows through the bank, and the nutrients new tissue
needs come out of the water and the bed where the bank buys it
([Nutrition](#nutrition)).

### The carbon–oxygen exchange

| Direction | Carbon | Oxygen |
|---|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | the Monod draw on `rateUnits × lightResponse × sufficiency × basePhotosynthesisRate × co2PerRateUnit`, read at the tick's closing CO₂ | derived from the carbon fixed, at `CO2_TO_O2_MASS_RATIO` |
| Respiration | `baseRespirationRate × rateUnits × warmth × air`, then `× co2PerRateUnit` | derived from the carbon released, same ratio |

One reaction run both ways, so one yield. The day/night asymmetry lives entirely
in `baseRespirationRate`, and the oxygen partner is not a second knob. A
carbon-starved tank stops producing oxygen because there was no carbon to pay
for it.

Respiration is quoted against the rate at saturating light and carbon; on the
4 mg/L an uninjected tank holds it is 4–9 % of what a plant actually fixes, at
or just below the bottom of the 5–15 % the macrophyte literature reports. A
planting under a fixture too dim to clear that respires more than it fixes and
draws oxygen with the lamps on.

### Stocks and rates

| Stock | Fills it | Drains it | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Units | Planting; a full bank's offshoot | Death; removal | plants |
| `size` | The bank, through the taper, at the species growth rate | Shedding at low condition; trimming | % of one full unit |
| `condition` | Benefits; healing from the bank | Stressors | %, normal at 100 |
| `surplus` | Income at full condition | Healing; an offshoot; growth | condition points, capped at `surplusCap` |
| `oxygen` | Photosynthesis | Respiration | mg/L |
| `co2` | Respiration | Photosynthesis, on its own Monod curve | mg/L |
| `nitrate` · `phosphate` · `potassium` · `iron` | Dosing; the bed's leak; nitrification for NO₃, and mineralized waste and fish excretion for the rest, elsewhere | New tissue — growth and offshoots — at the tissue recipe | mg |
| The bed's `nutrients` | Aqua soil and root tabs ([Equipment](/subsystems/equipment/#the-bed)) | Root feeders' new tissue, at the same recipe; the leak | mg |
| `waste` | Shed tissue and plant death, at the same grams growth drew for | Mineralization, elsewhere | g |

A bank unit is a condition point — the bank fills with the same %/h condition
is measured in, which is what lets one number pay for both.

### Nutrition

A plant feeds on two pools: the water column and the bed's store. Its growth
form's root share `r` fixes how much of its need it draws through its roots.
Every nutrient saturates on its own Monod curve in each pool, the two shares
weigh in by where the plant feeds, and sufficiency is the scarcest of them —
Liebig's law over saturating shares. Sufficiency sets what a plant earns
([Income and harm](#income-and-harm)).

```
share_p,n   = ppm_p,n / (demand_n × K_n + ppm_p,n)      p: the water, the bed
share_n     = r × share_bed,n + (1 − r) × share_water,n
sufficiency = min over n of share_n
```

The bed's ppm reads its store against the tank's capacity. A rooted plant over a
bare bed tops out at `1 − r` however rich the water, under the
[deficiency edge](#income-and-harm). A dry tank feeds nothing from either pool.

| Demand tier | NO₃ · PO₄ | K · Fe |
|---|---|---|
| Low | 0.3 | 0.05 |
| Medium | 0.6 | 0.15 |
| High | 1 | 1 |

Demand scales how little a plant can make do with. An Anubias needs a twentieth
of a carpet's potassium and iron, so the trace fish food leaves in an undosed
tank goes a long way for it. No nutrient ever reads quite 100 %.

### A plant is made of its nutrients

Tissue is the organic matter it rots into. A size point of a species is
`fullRateUnits × tissuePerSize` grams of it, carrying food's recipe: the nitrogen
of `foodNitrogenFraction`, as the nitrate it ends up as, and `foodMineralContent`
of the rest. N:P ≈ 7 by mass is Redfield, so one recipe serves food, waste and
tissue.

```
grams_i       = (size bought_i + offshoot_i) × fullRateUnits × tissuePerSize
w_i,p         = 1 − r_i from the water, r_i from the bed
grams_p       = Σ w_i,p × grams_i
requested_p,n = Σ w_i,p × grams_i × share_i,p,n
K_eq,p,n      = pool_p,n × (grams_p / requested_p,n − 1)
delivered_p,n = monodUptake(pool_p,n, grams_p × recipe_n, K_eq,p,n)
met_p,n       = delivered_p,n / (requested_p,n × recipe_n)
supply_i,n    = Σ over p of w_i,p × share_i,p,n × met_p,n
supply_i      = min over n of supply_i,n
drawn_p,n     = Σ grams_i × supply_i × recipe_n × w_i,p × share_i,p,n × met_p,n / supply_i,n
```

The bank asks; the pools decide how much arrives. Each plant requests its
tissue from each pool at the weight it feeds there and its share of each
nutrient in it — the readings its sufficiency is made of. Each pool delivers
through `monodUptake` against the half-saturation `K_eq` at which one consumer
would request, at the tick's start, what the planting requests. In an ordinary
hour a pool meets every request and a plant grows at its sufficiency; an
offshoot's lump on thin water is met in part, and no pool goes below zero. A
lean species holds on where a hungry one starves, and two plants short of the
same nutrient are met at the same fraction of their shares. The bloom requests
beside the plants in the same call ([Algae](/subsystems/algae/)), so nothing is
served before anything else. What a plant takes splits between the pools as
they met it. The bank pays only for what arrived.

Shedding and death hand the same grams back as waste, which mineralizes beside
its ammonia ([Nitrogen cycle](/subsystems/nitrogen-cycle/)) — into the water,
whichever pool the tissue came from. A tank that dies back releases exactly
what its plants took. Calcium and magnesium are drawn from the water on their
own Monod curve, off the nitrate actually drawn from either pool (under Key
tunables), and are not returned.

### Income and harm

Every benefit channel is realised *through* photosynthesis, so all three run on
its drive — the light term at the plant's mean leaf times its sufficiency —
rather than standing beside it: three peaks of `0.125` %/h make a budget of
`0.375` %/h × `tanh(PAR / Ik)` × sufficiency, and the dark or a nutrient run dry
takes the whole of it down together. Good water is worth nothing at midnight or
to an unfed plant — and poor water costs nothing in the dark either: the
deficiency stressor rides the light term, since a plant in the dark asks for
nothing. A plant's vigour multiplies all three too: hardiness scales what harms
a plant, vigour what it earns.

| Channel | Earns when | At saturating light |
|---|---|---|
| CO₂ | scales with the species' `co2Factor` | `co2BenefitPeak × co2Factor` |
| Temperature | inside `tolerableTemp`, most at its centre | `temperatureBenefitPeak × comfort` |
| pH | inside `tolerablePH`, most at its centre | `phBenefitPeak × comfort` |

```
// a symmetric parabola: 1 at the centre, 0 at either edge
comfort = 4 (x − lo)(hi − x) / (hi − lo)²
earned  = channel × tanh(PAR_i / Ik) × sufficiency_i × (1 + vigour)
```

| Stressor | Charges | Per |
|---|---|---|
| Light starvation | the day's light at the mean leaf short of the species' edge | share of the edge |
| Light high | PAR at the crown top over the band's high end, while the lamp is on | PAR |
| Temperature · pH · GH | outside the species band | °C · pH unit · dGH |
| Nutrient deficiency | sufficiency short of `sufficiencyEdge`, on the light term | share of the edge |
| Nitrate | past the plant's own edge | e-fold over |
| Algae shading | bloom mass over `algaeShadingThreshold`, not light ([Status](#status)) | mass point |

A Monod sufficiency never reaches 1, so deficiency starts under an edge rather
than under full: `sufficiencyEdge`, where a plant has 90 % of its need met. The
edge tops out at `MAX_SUFFICIENCY_EDGE`, 0.95: the ppm that meets it,
`demand_n × K_n × edge / (1 − edge)`, runs away as it nears 1.
Hardiness scales every stressor by `1 − hardiness` except nitrate, whose edge it
carries out by the same factor a fish's is: `nitrateEdge × 2^hardiness`.

### Starving on the day's light

```
shortfall = max(0, 1 − dli_i / edge)
damage    = lightStarvationSeverity × shortfall
            × q10(temperature) × (1 − hardiness)
```

`dli_i` is the day's light at the plant's mean leaf and `edge` the species'
edge, both as [Light](/subsystems/light/#the-days-light) defines them.

A plant starves on the light it has had, not the light it has now, once that
integral drops under its edge. The cost is respiration's, on the same Q10, so a
warm blackout bites harder than a cool one.

Starvation is damage like any other: the bank heals it first, then condition
falls, then the plant sheds. In a clean tank at 25 °C a monte carlo spends its
bank in the first days of total darkness and is gone in two to three weeks; a
java fern, hardier and needing less light, lasts about five.

### The bank

| Flow | Per hour | When |
|---|---|---|
| Banking | income past full condition, up to `surplusCap` | condition at 100 |
| Healing | `1 − e^−(healingDrawRate × growthRate)` of the bank, up to the deficit | condition under 100 |
| Offshoot | what the new unit's size costs at the growth conversion | the bank full |
| Growth | `growthDrawRate × (1 − size / 100)` of the bank, at the share the water and the bed supply | every hour, day and night |

A plant arrives at full condition with an empty bank, as a fish does. It heals
at the pace it grows: under heavy damage a carpet draws its bank down on repair
in about half a day, an anubias in about three.

### Growth and its taper

```
drawn = bank × growthDrawRate × (1 − size / 100) × supply
size += drawn × growthRate × sizePerSurplus
bank −= drawn
```

`supply` is the share of the hour's tissue the water and the bed deliver
([A plant is made of its nutrients](#a-plant-is-made-of-its-nutrients)). The
taper closes the draw as a unit fills. A young unit spends its bank on
tissue; a nearly full one draws almost nothing, so size approaches 100 and never
reaches it, and nothing clamps it. Only what becomes tissue leaves the bank, so
a growing plant keeps a day or two of income banked against a bad spell, and a
nearly full one banks to the cap, where the bank buys an offshoot instead.

Growth is absolute — a share of one unit an hour, not a share of the plant. A
planting compounds by adding units.

### Light at its own height

A plant reads light at its own height, through the water and the crowns above
its leaves — the [canopy](/subsystems/light/#the-canopy) — and the thinner its
own crown, the more of that light its leaves get.

That gives a starved plant a size it can hold. A plant too big for its light
drops under full condition and sheds; its crown thins, its leaves read more
light, and it stops where a day's income meets a day's damage. A plant with
light to spare banks, and the bank buys size that shades it again. Between the
two it settles at condition 100 — smaller, not dead. Only light too dim for even
a sparse unit to break even kills it.

### Crowding and carrying capacity

Every other crown that reaches above a plant's mean leaf shades it, at odds set
by that crown's share of the floor ([the canopy](/subsystems/light/#the-canopy)):
a tall planting darkens what grows under it, and carpet patches side by side
shade each other. Shedding relieves only a plant's own shade, so an understory
plant under taller crowns stays in their shade as it shrinks.
A planting therefore spreads until its lowest units sit at break-even — short
units lose first, and the tank carries as much leaf as its light holds. An
overgrown tank is dark, not dead.

| Reading | Is | The keeper lowers it by |
|---|---|---|
| Floor cover | every unit's footprint over the floor, `Σ footprint / (2 × depth²)`; past 1 the planting has outgrown its floor | thinning — removing units |
| Floor shade | the share of the substrate light the canopy takes before the floor ([Light](/subsystems/light/#the-canopy)) | trimming or thinning |

### Offshoots

After vitality settles, a plant whose bank is full buys an offshoot, before the
hour's growth.

```
offshoot.size = min(100, bank × growthRate × sizePerSurplus) × supply
bank         −= offshoot.size / (growthRate × sizePerSurplus)
```

- **A new unit of the family.** Its parent's species and family, at full condition on an empty bank — only a full plant holds a full bank — with its own vigour. It acts from the next hour.
- **Value is conserved** at the growth conversion: the parent pays for exactly the size it bought. At the shipped cap of 50, on full supply, a full bank buys an anubias of 6, a java fern of 10, a sword of 20, a hairgrass patch of 30 or a monte carlo patch of 36, and empties. On short water it buys a smaller one and keeps the change.
- **Propagation never tests space.** Offshoots land whatever the floor cover; crowding and the canopy decide which of them live.

The form sets the offshoot's shape and the words its `plant-propagated` log
entry uses — "Amazon Sword threw a plantlet". Every form buys it the same way.

### Lineage, family, age and vigour

| Field | Is |
|---|---|
| `parentId` | The unit whose offshoot this is; null for anything planted or seeded |
| `familyId` | The founder's id, inherited down the line, so a family outlives its founder |
| `age` | Hours in this tank. A seeded plant can start older. No rate reads it |
| `vigour` | An offset on income, drawn once at birth within ±`VIGOUR_SPAN` (`0.15`) — clonal ramets differ in growth by 10–20 % |

Vigour multiplies the three benefit channels, so a vigorous unit also heals
faster; growth and the offshoot's size carry none, so the conversion stays
exact.

### The floor budget and trim

The keeper plants by floor. A new unit fits when its footprint fits the floor
the planting leaves free, and each unit claims its form's full footprint at any
size — the territory a keeper spaces for — so seedlings can't pack a tank. An
epiphyte on the rock claims its plan view like any other.

Trimming cuts units down to a size: one unit, one family, or every plant above
the target. It lowers leaf area, and the height of anything but a carpet, and it
reopens the taper — the bank goes back into regrowth and spreading pauses. Trim
for light, or let the plant spread. Which units a thinning pulls is the keeper's
choice. The verbs are on [Actions](/subsystems/actions/).

### Shedding and death

```
deficit   = 1 − condition / 100
sizeLost  = maxSheddingRate × deficit² × size
waste    += sizeLost × fullRateUnits × tissuePerSize
```

Shedding is what low condition does to a plant. Squared in the deficit it is
0.5 %/h at condition 50 and 0.08 %/h at 80, so a plant relit or cleaned up before
its condition collapses keeps most of itself. The tissue leaves as waste rather
than as fuel — a failing plant drops its oldest leaves rather than digesting
them, which is why melting plants foul the water.

A plant dies at condition 0, as a fish does. What remains becomes waste, at
`size × fullRateUnits × tissuePerSize`, and it leaves the array, so a melting
sword fouls the water more than a carpet patch of the same size.
