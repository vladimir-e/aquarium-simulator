## What it models

Each plant is one unit of its growth form — a carpet patch, a sword, a rhizome
clump — with a condition and an energy budget, on the
[vitality](/concepts/vitality/) model. It reads light at its own height, under
whatever crowns and green water stand above it and through the film on its
leaves, and what that light fixes fills its reserve. Uptake fills four nutrient
stores from the water and the bed. The reserve pays maintenance first, then
growth, paced by the emptiest store, and a plantlet built on the parent and
cut loose at its form's size. Low condition sheds tissue.

## How it works

### The species roster

Five species, each of one [growth form](#growth-forms), in three tables: how
each grows, the light it lives on, and the water it tolerates.

| Species | Growth rate | CO₂ need | Nutrient demand | Hardiness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anubias | 0.3 | low | low | 0.75 |
| Java Fern | 0.5 | low | low | 0.7 |
| Amazon Sword | 1.0 | medium | medium | 0.5 |
| Dwarf Hairgrass | 1.5 | high | high | 0.3 |
| Monte Carlo | 1.8 | high | high | 0.3 |

Anubias and java fern attach to the scape; the sword and the carpets root in
the bed.

| Monte Carlo | 30–200 | 60 | 0.86 |

PAR is in µmol/m²/s and the rated day — the day of light the species is rated
to live on — in mol/m²/d. Both derive from the band's low end —
[saturation](/subsystems/light/#saturation) and
[the day's light](/subsystems/light/#the-days-light) on Light.

| Species | Temperature | pH | GH |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anubias | 18–30 °C | 5.0–8.0 | 1–20 dGH |
| Java Fern | 18–30 °C | 5.0–8.0 | 1–20 dGH |
| Amazon Sword | 20–28 °C | 5.5–7.8 | 2–20 dGH |
| Dwarf Hairgrass | 20–28 °C | 5.0–7.8 | 1–18 dGH |
| Monte Carlo | 20–28 °C | 5.0–7.8 | 1–15 dGH |

pH and GH bands are the generous published ranges, wide enough for aqua soil under CO₂ and hard tap alike. Past an edge the damage grows linearly with the distance, per °C, pH unit or dGH (`temperatureStressSeverity`, `phStressSeverity`, `ghStressSeverity`). pH is already a log scale, so linear in pH is log in acidity. Temperature and pH also earn inside their bands; GH is damage alone.

### Growth forms

A plant's `size` is how full one unit of its growth form is, in % — 100 is a
grown patch, specimen or clump — read off the tissue its budget holds. The form
fixes what that means.

| Form | Carpet | Rosette | Attached |
|---|---|---|---|
| Species | Dwarf Hairgrass, Monte Carlo | Amazon Sword | Anubias, Java Fern |
| One unit is | a patch | a specimen | a rhizome clump |
| Full height | 5 cm | 40 cm | 20 cm |
| Height grows as | full at any size — it spreads across | `size^⅓` | `size^⅓` |
| Footprint | 100 cm² | 700 cm² | 200 cm² |
| Leaf area index | 2.5 | 2.0 | 2.5 |
| Rate units at full | 0.5 | 2.8 | 1.0 |
| Plantlet | a runner rooting a tuft, 5 % of a patch | a runner plantlet, 10 % | a rhizome branch on three or four fronds, 25 % |
| Root share `r` | 0.5 — half from each | 0.7 — feeds mostly from the bed | 0 — feeds from the water |

Leaf area is the form's leaf area index over its footprint, in proportion to
size, and a rate unit is 500 cm² of it, weighing 3 g of organic matter. Size
says how full the unit is: it drives growth's taper, the height and the
self-shading. Leaf area says how much plant there is: it drives every flux the
tank sees, and the plant's metabolism runs it at its growth rate. No crown
stands taller than the water.

### Photosynthesis

Each plant's carbon capacity is its leaf in rate units at its growth rate, times
the light response at its mean leaf and its oxygen pace. The planting draws on
the dissolved CO₂ together, through one Monod at the capacity-weighted mean of
its half-saturations, so a dense planting slows its own draw and never takes
the water's last milligram. Species differ in how much carbon they need to run
near their ceiling, not in a floor that kills:

| CO₂ need | On 4 mg/L | On 25 mg/L |
|---|---|---|
| low | 73 % | 94 % |
| medium | 57 % | 89 % |
| high | 33 % | 76 % |

So a carpet in an uninjected tank grows slowly on a thin carbon income, and a
sword or an epiphyte hardly notices the injector.

What is offered fills the reserve, and only what the reserve takes is fixed:
a plant with a full reserve takes only what it burns, so a plant whose stores
can't pay for growth fills with carbon it cannot spend and draws little CO₂.

### The carbon–oxygen exchange

| Direction | Carbon | Oxygen |
|---|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | the CO₂ fixed into what the reserve takes | derived from the carbon fixed, at `CO2_TO_O2_MASS_RATIO` |
| Respiration | everything the budget burns — maintenance, the overhead of building, repair, tissue burned starving | derived from the carbon released, same ratio |

One reaction run both ways, so one yield, and the oxygen partner is not a
second knob. A carbon-starved tank stops producing oxygen because there was no
carbon to pay for it.

Maintenance is quoted against the rate at saturating light and carbon; on the
4 mg/L an uninjected tank holds it is 4–9 % of what a plant actually fixes, at
or just below the bottom of the 5–15 % the macrophyte literature reports. A
planting under a fixture too dim to clear that burns more than it fixes and
draws oxygen with the lamps on.

A plant is planted fed and stocked, as a shop sells it: its reserve full, every
store full, nothing toward a plantlet, at full condition.

### The budget

The plant's hour is a Dynamic Energy Budget (DEB, Kooijman) on Droop cell
quotas, the same one [a fish's](/subsystems/livestock/#the-budget) is, with
nutrient stores beside the carbon one, paced by the hour's Q10.

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Uptake | Each store asks what it lacks of full, closing first order at `storeFillDays` from saturating water; the pools deliver at their own Monod share ([Uptake](#uptake)) |
| Photosynthesis | Fills the reserve, up to `reserveCapacity` grams a gram of tissue |
| Mobilisation | The reserve releases at DEB's rate — a fuller reserve releases more — and the oxygen pace slows it in water short of air |
| Soma | `somaShare`, κ, of the release pays maintenance first, then builds tissue at its price, `1 / growthEfficiency` grams of reserve a gram — through the taper and at the Droop limit, taking its Q_min out of the stores |
| Plantlet | The other share builds the next unit in the offshoot buffer, at the same price and Droop limit |
| Shortfall | Maintenance the soma's share cannot pay is drawn from the plantlet's share, then its buffer, then tissue burned — and the share left unpaid is a damage rate |
| Repair | The reserve pays for the hour's repair at the plant's pace ([Vitality](/concepts/vitality/#one-hour)) |

What growth and the plantlet can't use stays in the reserve, so a plant whose
stores run low holds its photosynthate rather than spending it. The hour is
integrated in slices, each reading its own reserve and stores, so the budget
holds at any pace.

The **Droop limit** is each store's density read as a Monod against Q_min, the
emptiest store ruling. It never reaches 1: on full stores a plant runs at about
0.73, nitrogen ruling. Growth runs at it, and income is earned on it.

The **taper** is the room a unit has left: growth slows as it fills and stops at
100, so size approaches full and nothing clamps it. Leaf area scales with
tissue, so growth runs in proportion to the plant — DEB's V1-morph — and a
young unit grows on a logistic toward full. A planting compounds by adding
units.

### Uptake

A plant feeds on two pools: the water column and the bed's store. Its growth
form's root share `r` fixes how much of its stores it fills through its roots.
Every form a pool holds saturates on its own Monod curve, at the plant's demand
tier times the form's half-saturation, and the two pools weigh in by where the
plant feeds.

Ammonia goes straight into protein where nitrate has to be reduced first, so a
plant takes ammonia first, half-saturating at a twentieth of nitrate's ppm, and
asks nitrate only for what ammonia leaves, in the same hour. The bed holds its nitrogen as
nitrate, and its ppm reads its store against the tank's capacity. A dry tank
feeds nothing from either pool.

| Demand tier | NH₃ and NO₃ · PO₄ | K · Fe |
|---|---|---|
| Low | 0.3 | 0.05 |
| Medium | 0.6 | 0.15 |
| High | 1 | 1 |

Demand scales how little a plant can make do with. An Anubias fills its
potassium and iron stores at half pace on a twentieth of the ppm a carpet
needs, so the trace fish food leaves in an undosed tank goes a long way for it.

The stores ask; the pools decide how much arrives. Each pool delivers each form
through one Monod over every request on it, plants and blooms in the same call
([Algae](/subsystems/algae/)), so nothing is served before anything else, every
request is met at the same fraction of its share, and no pool goes below zero.

A plant keeps taking up past need until its stores are full — luxury uptake.
Between doses a high-tech planting draws 0.2–1 ppm of nitrate a day, and a
lightly stocked planted tank runs its water down to no nitrate or phosphate.

Calcium and magnesium are drawn from the water on their own Monod curve, off
the nitrogen actually taken from either pool, counted as nitrate (under Key
tunables), and are not returned. The nitrogen moves KH as it goes in: a proton
out per ammonia, one in per nitrate
([Water & gases](/subsystems/water-and-gases/#kh-and-ph)).

### Composition

Tissue is the organic matter it rots into, carrying Q_min of each nutrient —
the subsistence quota, the culture floors of the macrophyte literature — and the
stores hold what is taken up above it, up to Q_max. Food's 5 % nitrogen is a
macrophyte's luxury top; a plant's floor is a fraction of it.

| | N | P | K | Fe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q_min, mg/g | 12 | 0.8 | 4 | 0.05 |
| Q_max, mg/g | 45 | 6 | 25 | 0.25 |

Every species shares one composition, its sources cited beside the values in
the config; each kind of bloom carries its own
([Algae](/subsystems/algae/#key-tunables-and-traits)).

### Income and harm

Every benefit channel is earned on the plant's reserve density times its Droop
limit: good water is worth nothing to a plant that has burned its reserve or
run its stores dry. Three peaks of `0.125` %/h make a budget of `0.375` %/h on a
full reserve, a Droop limit of 1 and band centre. Light reaches income through
the reserve it fills, so a fed plant earns through the night.

| Channel | Earns when | At its best |
|---|---|---|
| CO₂ | scales with the species' carbon Monod | `co2BenefitPeak` |
| Temperature | inside `tolerableTemp`, most at its centre, nothing at its edges | `temperatureBenefitPeak` |
| pH | inside `tolerablePH`, most at its centre, nothing at its edges | `phBenefitPeak` |

| Stressor | Charges | Per |
|---|---|---|
| Starvation | maintenance the hour left unpaid | share of the hour's upkeep |
| Light high | PAR at the crown top over the band's high end, while the lamp is on | PAR |
| Temperature · pH · GH | outside the species band | °C · pH unit · dGH |
| Nutrient deficiency | the Droop limit short of `droopEdge` | share of the edge |
| Nitrate | past the plant's own edge | e-fold over |

A Droop limit never reaches 1, so deficiency starts under an edge rather than
under full: `droopEdge`, where a plant's nitrogen store has fallen to a sixth of
full, near the critical 1.3 % of Gerloff & Krombholz. The edge tops out at
`MAX_DROOP_EDGE`, 0.55: film's full stores reach 0.575 on nitrogen, the lowest
of any flora, and an edge past it would harm a bloom however full. Hardiness
scales every stressor by `1 − hardiness` except nitrate, whose edge it carries
out by the same factor a fish's is.

### Starving in the dark

A plant starves on its reserve, not on the day: the reserve carries it through
the night, and in the dark it pays maintenance as long as it can. As it runs
low the soma's share stops covering maintenance: growth stops, and the share
unpaid harms the plant at `starvationSeverity` while the plantlet's share pays
it, then the plantlet's buffer, and last tissue burned for fuel. Budding stops
next, and low condition sheds.

| Stocked, in total darkness | Reserve under a tenth | Condition starts to fall | Dead |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monte Carlo | 3.2 d | 4.2 d | 17 d |
| Dwarf Hairgrass | 3.9 d | 5.0 d | 18 d |
| Amazon Sword | 5.8 d | 7.5 d | 26 d |
| Java Fern | 11.6 d | 15 d | 48 d |
| Anubias | 19 d | 25 d | 68 d |

Reserve turns over with growth rate and hardiness stretches the melt, so the
slow growers outlast the fast. The hobby's three-day blackout leaves even a
carpet's condition whole.

### Light at its own height

A plant reads light at its own height, through the water and the crowns above
its leaves — the [canopy](/subsystems/light/#the-canopy) — and the thinner its
own crown, the more of that light its leaves get. The blooms take their share on
the way ([Light](/subsystems/light/#the-blooms)): green water the more the deeper
a leaf stands, so a carpet goes dark before a sword, and film the same share of
every leaf, which costs a light-hungry plant more than one that lives on little.

That gives a starved plant a size it can hold. A plant too big for its light
cannot pay its upkeep: its reserve drains, the shortfall harms it, and it
sheds; its crown thins, its leaves read more light, and it stops where a day's
photosynthate meets a day's maintenance. A plant with light to spare grows, and
growth shades it again. Between the two it settles — smaller, not dead. Only
light too dim for even a sparse unit to break even kills it.

### Crowding and carrying capacity

Every other crown that reaches above a plant's mean leaf shades it, at odds set
by that crown's share of the floor ([the canopy](/subsystems/light/#the-canopy)):
a tall planting darkens what grows under it, and carpet patches side by side
shade each other. Shedding relieves only a plant's own shade, so an understory
plant under taller crowns stays in their shade as it shrinks.
A planting therefore spreads until its lowest units sit at break-even — short
units lose first, and the tank carries as much leaf as its light holds. An
overgrown tank is dark, not dead.

| Reading | Is | The keeper lowers it by |
|---|---|---|
| Floor cover | every unit's footprint over the floor, `Σ footprint / (2 × depth²)`; past 1 the planting has outgrown its floor | thinning — removing units |
| Floor shade | the share of the substrate light the canopy takes before the floor ([Light](/subsystems/light/#the-canopy)) | trimming or thinning |
| Floor light | the fixture's rating carried to the floor through the water, the green water and the canopy | trimming, thinning, or clearing green water |

### The plantlet

The reserve's other share, `1 − somaShare`, builds the next unit in the offshoot
buffer as growth builds the parent. Once the buffer holds a unit at its growth
form's plantlet size and the stores hold its Q_min, it is cut loose, after
vitality and losses settle.

- **A new unit of the family.** Its parent's species and family, at full condition, with its own vigour and its share of the parent's reserve and of the stores past Q_min, so parent and plantlet stand at one density of both. It acts from the next hour.
- **Cadence is the budget's.** Grown and fed freely, a carpet patch sends a runner every two days, a sword throws a plantlet every six, a java fern branches every month and an anubias every fifty days. In the setup tanks a sword parent buds about every 19 days, a java fern every 59 and an anubias every 85.
- **Propagation never tests space.** Plantlets land whatever the floor cover; crowding and the canopy decide which of them live.

The form sets the plantlet's shape and the words its `plant-propagated` log
entry uses — "Amazon Sword threw a plantlet". Every form builds it the same way.

### Lineage, family, age and vigour

| Field | Is |
|---|---|
| `parentId` | The unit whose plantlet this is; null for anything planted or seeded |
| `familyId` | The founder's id, inherited down the line, so a family outlives its founder |
| `age` | Hours in this tank. A seeded plant can start older. No rate reads it |
| `vigour` | An offset on its assimilation ceiling, drawn once at birth within ±`VIGOUR_SPAN` (`0.15`) |

Vigour scales the most a unit's leaves fix at any light, so clones side by side
grow and bud apart, by about as much as their vigour differs.

### The floor budget and trim

The keeper plants by floor. A new unit fits when its footprint fits the floor
the planting leaves free, and each unit claims its form's full footprint at any
size — the territory a keeper spaces for — so seedlings can't pack a tank. An
epiphyte on the rock claims its plan view like any other.

Trimming cuts units down to a size: one unit, one family, or every plant above
the target. It takes the budget down in proportion — tissue, reserve, stores and
the plantlet's buffer — lowers leaf area, and the height of anything but a
carpet, and reopens the taper, so the soma's share goes back into regrowth.
Trim for light, or let the plant spread. Which units a thinning pulls is the
keeper's choice. The verbs are on [Actions](/subsystems/actions/).

### Shedding and death

Shedding is what low condition does to a plant: a share of its tissue an hour,
`maxSheddingRate` times the square of the condition deficit — 0.5 %/h at
condition 50 and 0.08 %/h at 80, so a plant relit or cleaned up before its
condition collapses keeps most of itself. The tissue leaves as waste at Q_min,
with its share of the reserve; the store it held is resorbed and stays with the
plant. A failing plant drops its oldest leaves, which is why melting plants foul
the water.

A plant dies at condition 0, as a fish does, and leaves the array. Its tissue,
reserve and plantlet's buffer become waste, so a melting sword fouls the water
more than a carpet patch of the same size; its stores leach straight to the
water, nitrogen as nitrate, and KH falls by the proton per nitrogen that
nitrate's uptake took.
