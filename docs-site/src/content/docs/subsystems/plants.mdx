## What it models

Each plant is a specimen with a size, a condition and a bank, on the
[vitality](/concepts/vitality/) model. Light drives everything it earns — the
photosynthetic rate and every benefit channel run through the same saturating
PAR curve — and the day's light decides whether it starves. Income at full
condition banks; the bank heals the plant and buys its size, and low condition
sheds tissue.

## How it works

### The species roster

| Species | Tolerable PAR | Saturates at | Starves under | Growth rate | CO₂ need | Nutrient demand | Hardiness | Temperature | pH | GH | Substrate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Anubias | 8–70 | 16 | 0.23 | 0.3 | low | low | 0.75 | 18–30 °C | 5.0–8.0 | 1–20 dGH | attaches |
| Java Fern | 10–90 | 20 | 0.29 | 0.5 | low | low | 0.7 | 18–30 °C | 5.0–8.0 | 1–20 dGH | attaches |
| Amazon Sword | 20–120 | 40 | 0.58 | 1.0 | medium | medium | 0.5 | 20–28 °C | 5.5–7.8 | 2–20 dGH | sand |
| Dwarf Hairgrass | 25–200 | 50 | 0.72 | 1.5 | high | high | 0.3 | 20–28 °C | 5.0–7.8 | 1–18 dGH | aqua soil |
| Monte Carlo | 30–200 | 60 | 0.86 | 1.8 | high | high | 0.3 | 20–28 °C | 5.0–7.8 | 1–15 dGH | aqua soil |

pH and GH bands are the generous published ranges, wide enough for aqua soil under CO₂ and hard tap alike. Past an edge the damage grows linearly with the distance, per °C, pH unit or dGH (`temperatureStressSeverity`, `phStressSeverity`, `ghStressSeverity`). pH is already a log scale, so linear in pH is log in acidity. Temperature and pH also earn inside their bands; GH is damage alone.

Both light figures are derived from the band's low end, not declared.
Saturating irradiance is `saturationIrradianceFactor × tolerableLight[0]`, so a
plant at the bottom of its band runs at 46 % of its rate. The daily light it
starves under, in mol/m²/d, is that same PAR held for the 8-hour day care sheets
quote their bands for.

### Photosynthesis

```
lightResponse_i = tanh(PAR / Ik_i)
co2Factor_i     = CO₂ / (K_i + CO₂)        // K_i by the species' CO₂ need
potential_i     = (size_i / 100) × co2Factor_i × lightResponse_i
actual_i        = potential_i × sufficiency_i
```

Carbon saturates like every other limit in the engine. Species differ in how
much carbon they need to run near their ceiling, not in a floor that kills:

| CO₂ need | On 4 mg/L | On 25 mg/L |
|---|---|---|
| low | 73 % | 94 % |
| medium | 57 % | 89 % |
| high | 33 % | 76 % |

So a carpet in an uninjected tank grows slowly on a thin carbon income, and a
sword or an epiphyte hardly notices the injector.

Actual — the Liebig-gated rate — drives the gases; potential, the tissue light
and carbon would build, drives nutrient uptake. The carbon curve is applied to
the gases once, as a draw: the planting takes CO₂ on the same end-of-tick Monod
as the nutrients, at the capacity-weighted mean of its CO₂ half-saturations, so
a dense planting slows its own draw and never takes the water's last milligram. Both are summed across the
planting and multiplied by `basePhotosynthesisRate` to give the tank's rate in
*rate units*, where one unit is an hour of 100 % plant size at saturating carbon
and light.

Photosynthesis emits resource effects only. It never adds size directly: growth
flows through the bank, so nutrient health reaches biomass once, through the
deficiency stressor, and is never counted twice.

### The carbon–oxygen exchange

| Direction | Carbon | Oxygen |
|---|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | the Monod draw on `(size/100) × lightResponse × sufficiency × co2PerRateUnit`, read at the tick's closing CO₂ | derived from the carbon fixed, at `CO2_TO_O2_MASS_RATIO` |
| Respiration | `baseRespirationRate × (size/100) × warmth × air`, then `× co2PerRateUnit` | derived from the carbon released, same ratio |

One reaction run both ways, so one yield. The day/night asymmetry lives entirely
in `baseRespirationRate`, and the oxygen partner is not a second knob. A
carbon-starved tank stops producing oxygen because there was no carbon to pay
for it.

Respiration is quoted against the rate at saturating light and carbon; on the
4 mg/L an uninjected tank holds it is 4–9 % of what a plant actually fixes, at
or just below the bottom of the 5–15 % the macrophyte literature reports. A
planting under a fixture too dim to clear that respires more than it fixes and
draws oxygen with the lamps on.

### Stocks and rates

| Stock | Fills it | Drains it | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `size` | The bank, scaled by species growth rate and the asymptotic factor | Shedding at low condition; trimming | %, where 100 is a full specimen |
| `condition` | Benefits; healing from the bank | Stressors | %, normal at 100 |
| `surplus` | Income at full condition | Healing; growth | condition points, capped at `surplusCap` |
| `oxygen` | Photosynthesis | Respiration | mg/L |
| `co2` | Respiration | Photosynthesis, on its own Monod curve | mg/L |
| `nitrate` · `phosphate` · `potassium` · `iron` | Dosing; nitrification for NO₃, and mineralized waste and fish excretion for the rest, elsewhere | Uptake on the plant's own need, each nutrient on its own Monod curve | mg |
| `waste` | Shed tissue and plant death | Mineralization, elsewhere | g |

A bank unit is a condition point — the bank fills with the same %/h condition
is measured in, which is what lets one number pay for both.

### Nutrition

Every nutrient saturates on its own Monod curve, and sufficiency is the scarcest
of them — Liebig's law over saturating shares.

```
share_n     = ppm_n / (demand_n × K_n + ppm_n)
sufficiency = min over n of share_n
capacity_n  = Σ potential × demand_n × uptakePerRateUnit_n
uptake_n    = the Monod draw on capacity_n, read at the tick's closing stock
```

| Demand tier | NO₃ · PO₄ | K · Fe |
|---|---|---|
| Low | 0.3 | 0.05 |
| Medium | 0.6 | 0.15 |
| High | 1 | 1 |

Demand is one number per nutrient per tier, and it scales both how much a plant
takes and how little it can make do with. An Anubias needs a twentieth of a
carpet's potassium and iron, so the trace fish food leaves in an undosed tank
goes a long way for it. No nutrient ever reads quite 100 %.

Uptake follows the plant, not the bottle. Its ratio is the tissue the plant's
light and carbon would build, so dosing moves the water column toward whatever
the plants leave behind rather than in lock-step with them. Each nutrient is
drawn on its own curve: a carpet held back by iron keeps taking the nitrate and
phosphate it has water for. The planting draws as one consumer, at the
capacity-weighted mean of its half-saturations, on the same end-of-tick Monod
the nitrifiers use — so a pool the plants drain slows its own draw and never
goes below zero.

Phosphate, potassium and iron come back the way nitrogen does, all of what food
carries ([Livestock](/subsystems/livestock/)); waste mineralizes them beside its
ammonia. Their budget is food and fertilizer in, plant uptake and water changes
out. Calcium and
magnesium are drawn on their own Monod curve, off the nitrate a plant actually
draws (under Key tunables).

### Income and harm

Every benefit channel is realised *through* photosynthesis, so all four are
multiplied by the same light term rather than standing beside it. Good water is
worth nothing at midnight — and poor water costs nothing then either: the
deficiency stressor rides the same term, since a plant in the dark asks for
nothing.

| Channel | Earns when | At saturating light |
|---|---|---|
| CO₂ | scales with the species' `co2Factor` | `co2BenefitPeak × co2Factor` |
| Temperature | inside `tolerableTemp`, most at its centre | `temperatureBenefitPeak × comfort` |
| pH | inside `tolerablePH`, most at its centre | `phBenefitPeak × comfort` |
| Nutrients | scales linearly with sufficiency | `nutrientBenefitPeak × sufficiency` |

```
comfort = 4 (x − lo)(hi − x) / (hi − lo)²     // symmetric parabola: 1 at the centre, 0 at either edge
```

| Stressor | Charges | Per |
|---|---|---|
| Light starvation | the day's light short of the species' edge, below | share of the edge |
| Light high | PAR over the band's high end, while the lamp is on | PAR |
| Temperature · pH · GH | outside the species band | °C · pH unit · dGH |
| Nutrient deficiency | Liebig shortfall, on the light term | point of sufficiency |
| Nitrate | past the plant's own edge | e-fold over |
| Algae shading | bloom mass over `algaeShadingThreshold` | mass point |

Hardiness scales every stressor by `1 − hardiness` except nitrate, whose edge it
carries out by the same factor a fish's is: `nitrateEdge × 2^hardiness`.

### Starving on the day's light

```
dli       = Σ lightByHour × 3600 / 10⁶                 // mol/m²/d, the last 24 hours
edge      = tolerableLight[0] × 8 h, as a daily light integral
shortfall = max(0, 1 − dli / edge)
damage    = lightStarvationSeverity × shortfall × q10(temperature) × (1 − hardiness)
```

A plant starves on the light it has had, not the light it has now. Under a
steady schedule the integral reads the same at midnight as at noon, so a night
costs nothing. A dead fixture takes one lit hour out of the window each hour it
should have been on, and the plant starves once the integral drops under its
edge. The cost is respiration's, on the same Q10, so a warm blackout bites
harder than a cool one.

Starvation is damage like any other: the bank heals it first, then condition
falls, then the plant sheds. In a clean tank at 25 °C a monte carlo spends its
bank in the first days of total darkness and is gone in two to three weeks; a
java fern, hardier and needing less light, lasts about five.

### The bank

| Flow | Per hour | When |
|---|---|---|
| Banking | income past full condition, up to `surplusCap` | condition at 100 |
| Healing | `healingDrawRate × growthRate` of the bank, up to the deficit | condition under 100 |
| Growth | `growthDrawRate × (1 − size ⁄ maxSize)` of the bank, bought at `sizePerSurplus × growthRate` | every hour, day and night |

A plant arrives at full condition with an empty bank, as a fish does. It heals
at the pace it grows: a carpet draws its bank down on repair in about half a
day, an anubias over three. Only what becomes tissue leaves the bank for growth, so a
plant at its ceiling in perfect water banks everything it earns. A growing plant
never settles at `surplusCap`: the draw closes on the income, and the bank sits
at a day or two of it.

### Shedding and death

```
deficit   = 1 − condition / 100
sizeLost  = maxSheddingRate × deficit² × size
waste    += sizeLost × wastePerShedSize
```

Shedding is what low condition does to a plant. Squared in the deficit it is
0.5 %/h at condition 50 and 0.08 %/h at 80, so a plant relit or cleaned up before
its condition collapses keeps most of itself. The tissue leaves as waste rather
than as fuel — a failing plant drops its oldest leaves rather than digesting
them, which is why melting plants foul the water.

A plant dies at condition 0, as a fish does, or once shedding has left less than
`deathSizeThreshold` of it. Its remaining size becomes waste and it leaves the
array.

## Key tunables

| Tunable | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| `basePhotosynthesisRate` | Rate units per hour per 100 % plant size at full carbon and saturating light | `1.0` /hr |
| `saturationIrradianceFactor` | Multiple of a species' band low at which it saturates — the `Ik` of the light curve | `2.0` × band low |
| `lowCo2HalfSaturation` · `mediumCo2HalfSaturation` · `highCo2HalfSaturation` | CO₂ at which each carbon-need tier photosynthesises at half rate | `1.5` · `3` · `8` mg/L |
| `co2PerRateUnit` | Carbon one rate unit moves, in either direction | `30` mg |
| `uptakePerRateUnit` | What a full-demand plant takes per rate unit, NO₃ · PO₄ · K · Fe | `3` · `0.3` · `1.5` · `0.03` mg |
| `halfSaturation` | Where a full-demand plant runs at half on each, NO₃ · PO₄ · K · Fe | `2` · `0.1` · `1` · `0.02` ppm |
| `demand` | Each tier's share of the full need, per nutrient — the table under Nutrition | — |
| `baseRespirationRate` | Dark respiration as a share of the light-saturated rate | `0.03` /hr |
| `respirationQ10` | Factor respiration and light starvation multiply by per 10 °C | `2.0` |
| `respirationOxygenHalfSaturation` | O₂ at which respiration runs at half rate | `0.5` mg/L |
| `surplusCap` | Ceiling on the bank | `50` units |
| `healingDrawRate` | Share of the bank that heals condition each hour, per unit of species growth rate | `0.05` /hr |
| `growthDrawRate` | Share of the bank drawn toward new tissue each hour | `0.02` /hr |
| `sizePerSurplus` | Size gained per converted unit, before the species growth rate | `0.4` % |
| `lightStarvationSeverity` | Damage in a day without light, at the reference temperature | `0.3` %/hr |
| `lightExcessiveSeverity` | Damage per PAR over the species band | `0.015` %/PAR/hr |
| `nitrateStressSeverity` · `nitrateEdge` | Damage per e-fold of NO₃ past the edge, and where a hardiness-0 plant's edge sits | `1.0` %/e-fold/hr · `100` ppm |
| `maxSheddingRate` | Share of itself a plant drops per hour at condition 0 | `0.02` /hr |
| `nutrientDeficiencySeverity` | Damage per point of Liebig shortfall, at saturating light | `0.3` %/hr |
| `ghStressSeverity` | Damage per dGH outside the species' band | `0.1` %/dGH/hr |
| GH per nitrate drawn | Calcium and magnesium taken up per mg of nitrate actually drawn, off leaf-tissue ratios; a constant | `0.28` mg CaCO₃/mg |
| GH half-saturation | GH, as CaCO₃, at which that uptake runs at half; a constant | `1` mg/L |
| Benefit peaks (×4) | Recovery per channel at its best, before the light term. Four at `0.125` sum, at band centre, to the 0.5 %/h budget the recovery curves were pinned against | `0.125` %/hr |
| `deathSizeThreshold` | Size under which a shedding plant is gone | `10` % |
