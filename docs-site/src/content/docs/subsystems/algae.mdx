## What it models

The bloom is plant mechanics without a position. It has a condition, a bank
and a mass on the [vitality](/concepts/vitality/) model, and its light curve,
income, harms, shedding, bank and tissue recipe are the
[plants'](/subsystems/plants/), on the plants' constants and its own traits. It
has no height, footprint or offshoots: in their place is a habitat it fills.

Mass is how full that habitat is, 0–100. The habitat is the water column, so a
full bloom's tissue scales with the tank's litres. Clear and green are not
coded: plants beat a bloom or lose to it on the rates.

## How it works

### What feeds it and what harms it

| Channel | Side | Reads |
|---|---|---|
| CO₂ | income | Monod on dissolved CO₂ at `co2HalfSaturation` — far under any plant's, since algae take bicarbonate too |
| Temperature · pH | income, then harm | The plants' band helpers on its own bands: comfort peaks mid-band, damage starts at the edge |
| Light starvation | harm | The day's light over the column against its daily light edge, at respiration's Q10 and its growth rate — a bloom respires fast, so it starves fast |
| Nutrient deficiency | harm | Liebig sufficiency under `sufficiencyEdge`, on the light curve |
| Allelopathy | harm | `allelopathySeverity × Σ(plant rate units × condition/100) / litres` |

Every harm is hardened by the bloom's hardiness. Income runs only while the lamp
is on; the bank carries it through the night.

### The bank buys mass

The draw is the plants': `growthDrawRate` of the bank through the taper. Each
point drawn buys `growthRate × sizePerSurplus` percent of the mass standing, so a
small bloom multiplies and a full habitat buys nothing. On a lit day's income a
bloom doubles in two or three days.

### Light, gases, nutrients

The bloom lives through the whole column, so it reads the mean PAR over it —
Beer–Lambert averaged from surface to floor, a little above the substrate
figure. It saturates at low light.

Its tissue is organic matter at the plants' recipe. A gram of it is the same
rate units a gram of leaf is, run at the bloom's growth rate, so a dense bloom
strips CO₂ by day and draws oxygen down by night. Growth draws its N, P, K and
Fe from the water in the same pooled call as the plants; shed, dead and scrubbed
tissue goes back as waste, so a crashing bloom spikes ammonia as it rots.

### Blackouts

Starvation reads the day's light, so a scheduled night costs nothing. A dead
fixture starves the bloom as its day empties: the bank goes in a day and the
condition in another, and the bloom dies back — the hobby's blackout, which
costs a plant its bank and little else.

## Key tunables and traits

| Name | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `growthRate` | Bank-to-mass conversion, healing share and metabolic pace, on the plants' scale | `30` |
| `hardiness` | Scales every harm by `1 − hardiness` | `0.4` |
| `lowLight` | PAR at the bottom of its band: its daily light edge over a care-sheet day, and `Ik` at `saturationIrradianceFactor ×` it | `6` PAR |
| `nutrientDemand` | Its demand tier, as a plant's | low |
| Temperature · pH bands | Where comfort peaks and harm starts | `12–34` °C · `5.5–9.5` |
| `co2HalfSaturation` | CO₂ it fixes at half rate on | `0.3` mg/L |
| `tissuePerLitre` | Organic matter in a litre of full bloom | `0.03` g |
| `sporeRate` | Mass that lands an hour, through the taper | `0.002` |
| `allelopathySeverity` | Harm per rate unit of thriving plant per litre | `3` %/h |

The first six are traits, beside the plant species; the last four are tunables.
