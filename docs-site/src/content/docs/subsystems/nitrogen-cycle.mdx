## What it models

Organic matter rots to ammonia, ammonia becomes nitrite, nitrite becomes
nitrate. Two bacterial guilds run the last two stages, and both pay in dissolved
oxygen — 4.57 mg of O₂ per mg of nitrogen carried the whole way, three quarters
of it on the first step. The colonies grow on the work they actually do and fade
on a maintenance clock that never stops, so a biofilter is a stock the tank has
to keep earning.

## How it works

### The three stages

| Stage | Reaction | Run by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Rot | food, waste, mulm (g) → NH₃ + PO₄ + K + Fe (mg) + CO₂ | Decomposers, first order on each pool at what it holds — see [where the ammonia comes from](#where-the-ammonia-comes-from) |
| 2 · Ammonia oxidation | NH₄⁺ + 1.5 O₂ → NO₂⁻ + 2 H⁺ + H₂O | AOB, saturating on what is dissolved |
| 3 · Nitrite oxidation | NO₂⁻ + 0.5 O₂ → NO₃⁻ | NOB, saturating on what is dissolved |

Nitrogen is conserved atom for atom across the chain. Compound mass is not — it
grows with molecular weight, and every conversion reads its ratio from the
shared chemistry in `src/simulation/core/` so the whole engine agrees on the
arithmetic.

| Conversion | Factor | Derivation |
|---|---|---|
| NH₃ → NO₂⁻ | `2.702` | `MW_NO2 / MW_NH3` |
| NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻ | `1.348` | `MW_NO3 / MW_NO2` |
| O₂ spent per mg NH₃ | `2.819` | `1.5 × MW_O2 / MW_NH3` |
| O₂ spent per mg NO₂⁻ | `0.348` | `0.5 × MW_O2 / MW_NO2` |
| KH per mg NH₃ minted, as CaCO₃ | `+2.939` | `+1 × CACO3_PER_EQUIVALENT / MW_NH3` |
| KH per mg NH₃ oxidised, as CaCO₃ | `−5.877` | `−2 × CACO3_PER_EQUIVALENT / MW_NH3` |

The oxygen draw is derived from the nitrogen actually oxidised, never quoted
separately. A tank that cannot run the reaction does not pay for it.

### Stocks and rates

| Stock | Fills it | Drains it | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `food` | Feeding, at the food's composition | Fish eating; rot; settling into the bed | g |
| `waste` | Fish feces, dead fish and eggs, plant and bloom shedding and die-back, scrubbed film, a disturbed bed | Rot; settling into the bed | g |
| mulm | Food and waste settling; a fresh bed's own organics | Rot; a gravel vac; disturbance, back into `waste` | g |
| `ammonia` | Rot; fish gill excretion — a meal's excess and every gram burned | Plant and bloom growth, from what the colony left; AOB oxidation; water change removes proportional mass | mg |
| `phosphate` · `potassium` · `iron` | Rot, at what each pool holds; fish excretion; fertilizer dosing; the bed's leak | Plant and bloom growth; water change | mg |
| `nitrite` | AOB oxidation, × 2.702 | NOB oxidation; water change | mg |
| `nitrate` | NOB oxidation, × 1.348; fertilizer dosing; the bed's leak | Plant and bloom growth, for the nitrogen ammonia leaves; water change | mg |
| `aob` | Seeding trickle; logistic growth on utilization | Maintenance decay; surface-area cap | units of 10⁶ cells |
| `nob` | Seeding trickle; logistic growth on utilization | Maintenance decay; surface-area cap | units of 10⁶ cells |
| `oxygen` | Gas exchange, elsewhere | Aerobic rot, 32/12 of the carbon it oxidises; 2.819 mg per mg NH₃ and 0.348 mg per mg NO₂⁻ oxidised | mg/L |

A water change dilutes the three nitrogen compounds but leaves the food and
waste standing in the water; only a gravel vac takes organic matter out, and
only the mulm in the bed.

### Storage is mass, not concentration

Nitrogen compounds are stored as mass in mg, and a concentration is derived from
that mass and the standing water only where a threshold or a display needs one.
Storing the conserved quantity is what makes evaporation concentrate the tank for
free: same mass, less water, higher ppm, no code. Oxygen is the exception — it is
stored as a concentration, so both oxidation steps divide their oxygen bill by
standing water before pushing the effect.

On a drained tank that derivation returns 0, and both oxidation stages are gated
on `water > 0`: nitrifiers oxidise what is dissolved. Maintenance decay sits
outside that gate on purpose, so a colony in a drained tank dies back rather
than waiting.

### Processing capacity

```
capacity    = population × processingRate × warmth × air
consumed    = capacity × S / (K + S)         // S: concentration the tick ends on
utilization = consumed / capacity            // 0..1, dimensionless
```

Each guild saturates on its own substrate as well as on oxygen: AOB on total
ammonia, NOB on nitrite. Capacity is the maximum a colony reaches in a glut; in
ordinary water it runs at a Monod share of it, so a colony works in proportion
to what is dissolved rather than at a fixed pace up to its limit.

The curve is read at the concentration the tick ends on — the root of
`consumed = capacity × monod(standing − consumed)`. Read at the start instead, a
colony with more throughput per hour than the half-saturation mass would empty
the pool every tick and a cycled tank would read exactly zero. Read at the end,
uptake never exceeds either the stock or the capacity, and a steady state rests
where the continuous curve puts it.

| Guild | Substrate | Half-saturation `K` | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| AOB | NH₃ + NH₄⁺, as NH₃ | `0.61` ppm (0.5 mg/L N) | *Nitrosomonas*, low end of wastewater models' 0.5–1 mg/L N |
| NOB | NO₂⁻ | `0.66` ppm (0.2 mg/L N) | *Nitrospira*, the guild that dominates aquarium biofilters, 0.1–0.3 mg/L N |

Capacity carries no volume term. Throughput is a property of the cell, so the
same colony clears the same milligrams in a nano and in a 150 L — which is what
makes a ppm reading fall with volume the way it does in a real tank.

NOB take the same per-cell throughput scaled by ≈ `2.951`. That is the 2.702 mass ratio
re-quoted through each guild's own oxygen factor at air saturation, rather than
the bare ratio, which would hand NOB AOB's correction on top of their own and
leave them 8.4 % under parity.

### Colony dynamics

| Flow | Form | Gated on |
|---|---|---|
| Seeding | `water × seedingRate`, both guilds | Standing water; held under the surface ceiling |
| Growth | `pop × growthRate × warmth × air × utilization × (1 − pop/max)` | Utilization — nothing to oxidise means no growth |
| Decay | `pop × deathRate × warmth` | Nothing. It is unconditional |
| Surface cap | Population truncated to `surface × bacteriaPerCm2` | Surface leaving the tank — a rescape, a media change |
| Disturbance | The disturbed share of the bed's colony scraped off; a lifted piece takes its own biofilm | A keeper digging into the bed |

Seeding is how a colony starts: cells settle out of the fill water and the air
every tick, so a guild is never absent and emerges on exponential growth the
moment its feedstock appears. With nothing to eat, a guild idles at
`seedingRate / deathRate` — about 73 cells/mL. The rate is the cycling clock,
and logarithmic in it: each tenfold cut adds three to four days.

`air` scales oxidation and growth alike, because a colony cannot divide on a
reaction it cannot run. It deliberately does not scale maintenance decay, which
is what makes an anoxic tank lose its biofilter rather than merely pause it.

Decay is maintenance loss, not starvation. Bacteria fade over weeks rather than
collapsing when the meal ends, which is why a tank survives a holiday.

A colony under a steady load settles where the two flows cancel:

```
utilization_rest = deathRate / (growthRate × air × (1 − pop/max))
```

In air-saturated water with the ceiling far off, that is **4.0 % for AOB** and
**7.1 % for NOB**. The rest point moves with oxygen, and not with temperature —
`warmth` cancels out of it, because a cell that oxidises half as fast also
divides and starves half as fast.

Utilization is the substrate curve, so the rest point fixes the concentration a
cycled tank holds: `S = K × u / (1 − u)` — about 0.03 ppm ammonia and 0.05 ppm
nitrite, almost whatever the load. A heavier load grows a bigger colony, not a higher
reading. A pulse lifts `S` and with it the colony's pace, so it clears in hours
on a mature colony with headroom and in days on one still growing into its load.

### Oxygen: why nitrite stands

| Guild | Half-saturation `K` | Rate left at 8.38 mg/L | Rate left at 0.10 mg/L |
|---|---|---|---|
| AOB | `0.30` mg/L | 96.5 % | 25.0 % |
| NOB | `1.10` mg/L | 88.4 % | 8.3 % |

Every base rate multiplied by that factor is a Monod **maximum**, reached only
at infinite oxygen. The three nitrifier rates divide the shortfall back out, so
what a tank at air saturation reproduces is the quoted 20 h doubling rather than
the 20.7 h an uncorrected constant would give.

Both guilds read their oxygen where the hour leaves it, once rot, both
oxidations and the air have drawn on the one stock together
([Water & gases](/subsystems/water-and-gases/#ordering-within-the-tick)).
The ratio between the two guilds runs from `1.000` at air saturation to `0.364`
at 0.10 mg/L. That gap is the whole story of standing nitrite: a tank short of
air goes on oxidising its ammonia long after it has stopped clearing the nitrite
that ammonia becomes.

Oxygen, not surface, is the binding constraint on a mature colony. Held under a
saturating dose, a bare 200 L settles at 95 % of its surface ceiling for AOB and
53 % for NOB — against 96 % / 94 % with the oxygen term switched off.
Circulation decides how far short a guild stops: NOB run from 1.4 % with nothing
moving the water to 90.1 % on a canister, an air pump and a powerhead.

### Who else takes the ammonia

Plants and the blooms take their nitrogen as ammonia first
([Plants](/subsystems/plants/#uptake)), and they run in the living tier,
before the cycle: each hour they draw on what the colony left, and the colony
meets what they leave beside the hour's gill excretion; what rots lands beside
the colony's work and meets it the next hour. Each side draws through its own
end-of-tick Monod, so neither overdraws the stock.

Flora take ammonia only as their stores lack it, so beside a stocked tank's load
they are a small drain — about 1 % in a community tank with a few easy plants,
5–15 % in a planted one — and a colony that still gets the rest settles at its
usual trace. Where they take most of it, the colony shrinks to what is left and
ammonia falls under that trace, to where the flora's draw meets the supply. An
uncycled tank's rising ammonia feeds them fully.

### Temperature

```
warmth = q10 ^ ((temperature − referenceTemp) / 10)
```

One metabolism, one factor: oxidation, growth and maintenance all carry it. A
cold tank therefore needs a larger colony to clear the same load and takes
longer to build it — an 18 °C cycle runs about twice the days a 25 °C one does —
while the utilization the colony rests at does not move.

### Where the ammonia comes from

Rot and the fish. Every organic pool rots on one law: first order on what it
holds, Q10 on temperature, aerobic on a Monod of the oxygen there is, and a
slow anaerobic path, `anaerobicShare` of the pace, taking over as the oxygen
runs out. What rots releases what the pool is made of — its nitrogen as
ammonia, its phosphorus, potassium and iron to the water, its carbon as CO₂.
Food and waste also settle into the
bed as mulm, fastest in still water and halving at `settlingHalfTurnover` tank
turnovers an hour; a bare bottom holds none. Every pool moves its content with
its grams, so the nitrogen in the bed is the nitrogen that left the water.

| Pool | Where | Half-life, 25 °C, oxygenated |
|---|---|---|
| `food` | The water | ~14 h |
| `waste` | The water | ~1.5 d |
| mulm | The bed | ~10 d |

A fish excretes the rest at its gills: a meal's nitrogen past what its reserve
builds, and every gram of reserve it burns
([Livestock](/subsystems/livestock/#the-ledger)) — so a richer food makes more
ammonia.

A fresh bed's mulm rots down until what settles in balances what rots. A water
change can vacuum the bed, taking a share of its mulm out of the tank.

| Substrate | Colony surface | Organic reserve |
|---|---|---|
| `none` | `0` cm²/L | `0` g/L |
| `sand` | `400` cm²/L | `0.011` g/L |
| `gravel` | `800` cm²/L | `0.013` g/L |
| `aqua_soil` | `1200` cm²/L | `0.05` g/L |

Surface and reserve are separate properties of the same bed: surface is the
colony ceiling, the reserve is mulm, rotting.

Digging into the bed stirs a share of it: that share of the mulm comes loose
as waste, and that share of the bed's biofilm is scraped off.

| Move | Share of the bed disturbed |
|---|---|
| Uprooting a rooted plant | its footprint's share of the floor — `footprint / floor`; an epiphyte disturbs nothing |
| Lifting a piece of hardscape | one hardscape slot — `1 / slots`; the piece's own biofilm leaves with it |
| Setting a piece down | nothing; it arrives sterile |
| Swapping the bed | all of it — the colony on it goes out with it and the new bed brings a fresh reserve |

A release lifts the concentration the colony works against, so it stands as a
bump until worked down. An established bed holds only its mulm, so rescaping a
cycled tank shows as a small, brief bump; digging up most of the bed at once
shows more. An inert bed holds less mulm than soil does, so its colony grows to
a smaller load, but it cycles on the same seeding clock. A `'cycled'` seed skips
the clock: each guild opens at the population where growth on its resting load
meets decay, never below a fishless month's share of the ceiling. That load
counts the ration that pays the fish's upkeep, not food fed past it, so a
tank whose keeper feeds more grows its colony on from the seed.
