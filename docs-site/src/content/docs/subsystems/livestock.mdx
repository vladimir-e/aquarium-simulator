## What it models

Every fish is an individual whose body is its energy budget (DEB, Kooijman):
structure, reserve, maturity and a brood buffer, in grams. Food fills the gut;
what the gut digests feeds the reserve; the reserve pays maintenance first, and
a fixed share κ of the rest buys growth while the remainder matures the fish
and then fills its brood buffer. Mass and size are read off the budget. Health
is the damage axis: the water and the fish's own budget harm it, benefits and
paid repair restore it. Old age is DEB ageing — a hazard that accumulates into
a survival the fish dies under. A brood is a clutch of eggs the water and the
tank's fish thin, and the larger fish hunt the young that hatch.

## How it works

### The roster

Each species runs on its Add-my-Pet entry where the entry fits its own data;
otherwise on its closest relative's, rescaled by hobby length, weight,
maturity age and lifespan. Grown figures are a female fed without limit; ages
are read at the species' mid-range temperature, hatching at 25 °C.

| Species | Budget | Grown | Stocked (80 %) | Puberty | Median lifespan | Hatches in | Egg · hatchling size |

| Neon Tetra | entry | 2.6 cm · 0.22 g | 0.11 g · 199 d old | 57 d | 7.7 y | 7.7 d | 0.52 mg · 13 % |
| Guppy | entry | 5.7 cm · 1.1 g | 0.55 g · 511 d old | 79 d | 2.7 y | 27 d, carried | 2.7 mg · 13 % |
| Betta | proxy | 5.5 cm · 3.0 g | 1.5 g · 336 d old | 120 d | 3.0 y | 1.3 d | 0.011 mg · 1.5 % |
| Corydoras | entry | 7.8 cm · 3.1 g | 1.6 g · 218 d old | 165 d | 6.0 y | 3.2 d | 0.67 mg · 6 % |
| Angelfish | entry | 15 cm · 27 g | 14 g · 327 d old, juvenile | 392 d | 10.7 y | 5.6 d | 1.0 mg · 3 % |

pH and GH bands are generous hobby care-sheet ranges. Flow tolerance is a
turnover — flow divided by standing water — so a fixed-output powerhead is
harsher in a small tank.

### The budget

Every energy-like quantity is grams of organics; structure is a volume V in
cm³ with structural length L = V^⅓. Per hour, with τ the species' Arrhenius
factor (1 at 20 °C) and ω the oxygen pace:

| Flux | Per hour |
|---|---|
| Assimilation ceiling | `{p_Am} × L² × τ × ω` |
| Assimilated | `κ_X ×` what the gut offers, up to the ceiling; the rest of the offer returns to the gut |
| Mobilised | `E × (ω·v·[E_G]·L² + [p_M]·V) × τ / ([E_G]·V + κ·E)` |
| Maintenance | `[p_M] × V × τ`, paid from the soma share `κ × mobilised` |
| Growth | what the soma share leaves, at `[E_G]` a cm³; the overhead `1 − κ_G` is burned |
| Maturity | `k_J × E_H × τ` paid from the other share; the rest matures the fish until puberty, then fills the brood buffer |
| Shortfall | maintenance its share could not pay — drawn from the buffer, then from structure, which shrinks |

Wet mass is `V + (E + E_R) / d_V`; size is `100 × L / L_m`. A fish reads as an
adult once its maturity reaches puberty, at any size; short of it, as fry under
half its grown length and as a juvenile from there. Reserve density
`E / ([E_m] × V)`, capped at 1, is how well fed it is. ω is a Monod on dissolved
oxygen against saturated water, so a healthy tank reads 1; it scales only
supply, so a fish longer than `ω × L_m` cannot pay its maintenance however well
it is fed.

### Feeding and the gut

A fish's appetite is its assimilation ceiling in food, `ceiling / κ_X`; its
gut holds a day of it, so a fry eats more for its weight than its parents.
Every fish wants the room left in its gut; when food runs short each takes the
same share of its room. The gut digests first order at
`digestionRate × τ`. A stocked fish and a hatchling arrive with empty guts and
full reserves.

### The ledger

Digested food splits κ_X to reserve and the rest to feces, which carry the
food's nitrogen and minerals to the waste pool. Reserve leaves only as
structure, buffer or burn: maintenance, maturity upkeep and development, every
conversion overhead, repair, and structure a starving fish loses. Every gram
burned breathes `oxygenPerGramBurned` of O₂, exhales CO₂ at the respiratory
quotient, and excretes the food's nitrogen as gill ammonia with its minerals
beside it. A cm³ of structure holds `κ_G × [E_G]` grams. Eggs hold `E_0`; what
an embryo spends reaching its hatchling is burned at hatching. A stocked fish
brings its body in; a removed or sold one takes its body, gut and carried brood
out; a dead one leaves them all.

|---|---|---|---|

| `gut` | Eating, up to a day of appetite; what the budget hands back; eggs and fry it eats | Digestion, first order | g |
| `deb.structure` | Growth | A shortfall the buffer cannot pay | cm³ |
| `deb.reserve` | Assimilation | Mobilisation; repair | g |
| `deb.maturity` | The maturity share, until puberty | Unpaid maturity upkeep, short of puberty | g |
| `deb.broodBuffer` | The maturity share after puberty | Spawning; fathering; a male's turnover; a shortfall | g |

| clutch `development` | `τ / incubation` each hour | Hatching, at 1 | share of the way to hatching |
| `age` | One tick per hour | — | ticks |

### Health

Health moves by benefits less damage, plus repair: the fish asks
`(1 − e^−healingRate × τ) × (100 − health)` points an hour of its reserve at
`healingCost` hours of its somatic maintenance a point, paid at the hour's
oxygen; an empty reserve repairs nothing. Health above 100 is lost. A fish is
sick while its damage outruns what it earns and repairs.

| Stressor | Activates | Per |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | outside the species range | °C |
| pH | outside the species range | pH unit |
| GH | outside the species range | dGH |
| Free NH₃ | past the fish's own edge, of the *unionized* fraction read off pH and temperature | e-fold over |
| Nitrite | past the fish's own edge | e-fold over |
| Nitrate | past the fish's own edge | e-fold over |
| Oxygen | under the fish's own edge | e-fold under |
| Water level | below `waterLevelStressThreshold` of capacity | % under |
| Flow | above the species `maxTurnover` | tank volumes/h over |
| Starvation | maintenance the hour left unpaid | share of the hour's upkeep |
| Hunted | larger fish in the tank, on any fish short of grown length | g of them per litre |
| Age | always: `hazardSeverity × hazard` | — |

Hardiness scales stressors as `1 − hardiness`, except water quality, where it
moves the edge, and age, which nothing shields. Every benefit is its peak
times the fish's reserve density.

| Benefit | Earns when | Peak |
|---|---|---|
| pH | inside the species range, most at its centre and nothing at its edges | `phBenefitPeak` |
| Oxygen | rising on log scale from 4 mg/L, full from 6 | `oxygenBenefitPeak` |
| Plants | plant power, saturating at `plantBenefitSaturationPoint` | `plantBenefitPeak` |

#### Water quality

Toxicity runs on log dose. Each toxin stressor is its severity times
`ln(reading / edge)`; oxygen's is `ln((edge + a) / (reading + a))` with
`a` = 0.1 mg/L, so harm stays finite at zero. A fish's edge sits `2^hardiness`
past the alert's (below it, for oxygen), with effective hardiness clamped to
[0.1, 0.95]. Each severity is set so a mid-hardiness fish at its 96-hour LC50
loses one %/h net.

| Reading | Alert edge | A mid-hardiness fish is harmed from | Breaks even | LC50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free NH₃ | 0.02 ppm | 0.028 ppm | ≈ 0.17 ppm | ≈ 1 ppm |
| Nitrite | 0.5 ppm | 0.71 ppm | ≈ 2.7 ppm | ≈ 10 ppm |
| Nitrate | 80 ppm | 113 ppm | ≈ 300 ppm | ≈ 800 ppm |
| Oxygen | 4 mg/L | 2.8 mg/L | ≈ 2.2 mg/L | ≈ 1.5 mg/L |

#### Predation

Every larger fish hunts a smaller one by the grams it outweighs it.
Vulnerability reads the prey's size — its length against what its species
grows to — so only the young are hunted:

```
predator mass = Σ over every fish of max(0, its mass − prey mass)
hunted        = predationRate × predator mass / water × (1 − size / 100)^preyVulnerabilityExponent
```

hardened by `1 − hardiness`. A fed fry earning past it grows out of reach; a
hungry or crowded one dies — so a tank breeds until its own fish hold the fry
back.

#### Ageing

Wear accrues with mobilisation, `dq = (q × V/V_m × s_G + h_a × τ²) ×
mobilised / ([E_m] × V)`, and drives the hazard, `dh = q`, both diluted as
the fish grows. The hazard damages health at `hazardSeverity`, so an old fish
is frail and sickens first; and each hour survival keeps `e^−hazard` of
itself. A fish dies of old age when its survival falls below the threshold it
drew at creation, so a cohort follows its species' survival curve — half gone
at its median lifespan — and a fish kept warm ages faster.

### Individual variation

Sex, a hardiness offset of ±15 % of the species baseline, a small health
jitter and a survival threshold are sampled once at construction. Stocked and
hatched fish come from the same factory.

A stocked fish was grown in the shop: stocking names a size in % of grown
length — from its hatchling's, 80 by default, up to 90, since grown length is an
asymptote — and its budget is the one its species reaches at that size fed
without limit at its mid-range temperature, its broods spent along the way. Its
age, wear and survival are that upbringing's, whatever tank buys it. Its
threshold is drawn under the survival it arrives with. A hatchling starts at L_b
on a full reserve at E_Hb.

### Broods

Spawning comes in bouts. Eggs ready are `⌊κ_R × E_R / E_0⌋`; an egg-layer
spawns once they reach her species' clutch, a livebearer once a whole egg is
ready and only while she carries no brood, so she conceives what her buffer
gathered through the last gestation. She draws every egg ready. The males of
her species father as many as their buffers pay for at their species'
`maleShare` of an egg's cost, `maleShare × E_0 / κ_R`, shared back to each
ready female in proportion to her eggs, each male paying the same share of
what his could. Fathered eggs become her clutch; she resorbs the rest, and
resorbed eggs, the overhead `1 − κ_R` and the males' payment are burned. A
male burns his unspent buffer at `maleBufferTurnover × τ`, so he holds about
a week of it. A starving fish's buffer pays its maintenance first, so breeding
stops before it shrinks. Breeding is uncapped: the stocking ceiling gates
only the keeper's hand.

### Clutches

A clutch is a count of eggs, each holding `E_0` and weighing its hatchling.
Every hour two competing first-order losses thin it and one rate develops it:

| Rate | Per hour |
|---|---|
| Harm | `eggSensitivity × Σ water stressors / 100` at the species' hardiness; a carried brood's at its mother's hardiness, without `eggSensitivity` |
| Predation | `eggPredationRate × exposure × predator mass / water`, every fish by the grams it outweighs an egg; none on a carried brood |
| Development | `τ / incubation`, the species' age at birth at 20 °C |

At development 1 each whole egg left hatches; the part egg goes to waste.
Eggs the water kills go to waste; eaten eggs go into the eaters' guts by the
grams each outweighs an egg, as room allows, the rest to waste.

| Species | Mode | Clutch | Exposure | Male share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Guppy | livebearer | what her buffer holds | 0 | 0.2 |
| Neon Tetra | egg-scatterer | 80 | 1 | 0.2 |
| Betta | bubble-nester | 150 | 0.3 | 0.6 |
| Corydoras | egg-depositor | 20 | 1 | 0.2 |
| Angelfish | substrate-spawner | 300 | 0.5 | 0.5 |

Clutch is the hobby figure for the eggs a bout lays, the least an egg-layer's
buffer holds before she spawns. Exposure is how much of the tank's predation
reaches a laid clutch. A carried brood dies or leaves the tank with its mother.

### Death

Health reaching zero, or survival falling under the fish's threshold, removes
it. A death the survival threshold fired, or mostly of age damage, is logged as
old age; one mostly of starvation as starved; one mostly of predators as eaten.
Its remains — body, gut and any carried brood — rot to waste, less the share
its surviving predators eat: their share of its dying hour's damage, into their
guts by weight as room allows.
