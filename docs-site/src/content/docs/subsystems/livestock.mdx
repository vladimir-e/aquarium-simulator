## What it models

Every fish is an individual with a mass, a health, a gut and a bank. What the
gut digests is the fish's income, as nutrient sufficiency is a plant's: every
benefit is earned on it, and digesting less than it needs harms. Its whole
metabolism rides on one metabolic factor — temperature's Q10 times an oxygen
factor. Health is the [vitality model](/concepts/vitality/): income at full
health banks, and the bank heals the fish and buys its growth and broods. A
brood is a clutch of eggs the water and the tank's fish thin, and the larger
fish hunt the young that hatch.

## How it works

### The roster

| Species | Adult mass | Growth rate | Lifespan | Hardiness | Temperature | pH | GH | Max turnover |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Guppy | 1.0 g | 1 | 3 y | 0.8 | 22–28 °C | 6.5–8.5 | 6–25 dGH | 13 ×/h |
| Betta | 3.0 g | 1 | 3 y | 0.6 | 24–30 °C | 6.0–8.0 | 3–15 dGH | 5 ×/h |
| Corydoras | 4.0 g | 0.25 | 5 y | 0.7 | 22–26 °C | 6.0–8.0 | 2–15 dGH | 15 ×/h |
| Angelfish | 15.0 g | 0.2 | 10 y | 0.4 | 24–30 °C | 6.0–8.0 | 3–15 dGH | 10 ×/h |

pH and GH bands are generous hobby care-sheet ranges. Flow tolerance is a
turnover — flow divided by standing water — so a fixed-output powerhead is harsher
in a small tank.

### Metabolism

The metabolic factor is the Q10 term times the oxygen Monod factor, read once an
hour for the whole tank. Its half-saturation sits at 1.0 mg/L, the highest in the
tank, so fish feel a shortage before the bacteria do.

A fish's rates run on its metabolic mass, not its mass: Kleiber's law puts a
body's metabolism on mass to the ¾, so per gram it runs on mass to the −¼.
Against a grown fish of its own species, `M = adultMass × (mass / adultMass)^(1 + massScalingExponent)`,
so a grown fish sits at the per-gram constants and a guppy fry at 1 % of adult
mass holds, needs, breathes and asks to grow 3.2× as much per gram.

| Flow | Per hour | Scaled by the metabolic factor |
|---|---|---|
| Digested | `gut × (1 − e^−k)`, `k = digestionRate × metabolic factor` | yes |
| Maintenance | `maintenanceRation × M / 24 × metabolic factor` | yes |
| Eaten | the room left in the gut, `gutCapacity × M − gut`, or its share of short food | no |
| Oxygen drawn | `baseRespirationRate × M × metabolic factor` | yes |
| CO₂ exhaled | the oxygen drawn × `respiratoryQuotient`, through the molar mass step | yes — it derives from the draw |
| Gill ammonia | (digested × `assimilatedFraction` − retained) × `foodNitrogenFraction`, as NH₃ | through digestion |
| Feces | digested × `(1 − assimilatedFraction)` | through digestion |
| Minerals, beside the gill ammonia | (digested × `assimilatedFraction` − retained) × `foodMineralContent` | through digestion |

A gram of fish, egg or embryo is `bodyOrganicShare` grams of organic matter
of the food's recipe — 2.75 % N and 0.36 % P by wet mass — and water. Every
milligram fed returns to the water or stays in a body: digested, through the
gills or feces, less what growth retains; uneaten, as it decays; at death, the
whole body, gut and brood. A stocked fish brings its body and gut in; a removed
or sold one takes them out.
The oxygen stressor is a separate reading, so a suffocating fish draws less
*and* suffers more.

### Feeding and the gut

Every fish wants the room left in its gut. While food lasts every appetite is
met; when it runs short each fish takes the same share of its appetite. Food
left over decays. A stocked fish arrives with a day's maintenance ration in its
gut; a hatchling's yolk is the same ration.

Digestion is the fish's income. Against its maintenance ration —
`maintenanceRation × M` a day, scaled by the metabolic factor — it sets two
rates:

| Rate | Shape | At maintenance |
|---|---|---|
| Nourishment | `digested / (digested + maintenance)`, scaling every benefit | half |
| Hunger | `hungerSeverity × (1 − digested / maintenance)` below it, hardened | nothing |

A ration past maintenance banks faster, grows sooner and buys more broods; one
under it earns less and harms.

### Health

A fish heals out of its income first, then out of its bank, and banks only what
full health leaves over. It is sick while damage outruns healing.

| Stressor | Activates | Per |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | outside the species range | °C |
| pH | outside the species range | pH unit |
| GH | outside the species range | dGH |
| Free NH₃ | past the fish's own edge, of the *unionized* fraction read off pH and temperature | e-fold over |
| Nitrite | past the fish's own edge | e-fold over |
| Nitrate | past the fish's own edge | e-fold over |
| Oxygen | under the fish's own edge | e-fold under |
| Water level | below `waterLevelStressThreshold` of capacity | % under |
| Flow | above the species `maxTurnover` | tank volumes/h over |
| Hunger | digesting under its maintenance ration | share short of it |
| Wear | always, doubling every `wearDoublingShare` of the species lifespan | — |
| Hunted | larger fish in the tank, on any fish short of adult size | g of them per litre |

Hardiness scales stressors as `1 − hardiness`, except water quality, where it
moves the edge. Every benefit is its peak times the fish's nourishment, and
their sum in a clean tank is the headroom a fed fish banks from.

| Benefit | Earns when | Peak |
|---|---|---|
| pH | inside the species range, most at its centre and nothing at its edges | `phBenefitPeak` |
| Oxygen | rising on log scale from 4 mg/L, full from 6 | `oxygenBenefitPeak` |
| Plants | plant power, saturating at `plantBenefitSaturationPoint` | `plantBenefitPeak` |

#### Water quality

Toxicity runs on log dose. Each toxin stressor is its severity times
`ln(reading / edge)`; oxygen's is `ln((edge + a) / (reading + a))` with
`a` = 0.1 mg/L, so harm stays finite at zero. A fish's edge sits `2^hardiness`
past the alert's (below it, for oxygen), with effective hardiness clamped to
[0.1, 0.95]. Each severity is set so a mid-hardiness fish at its 96-hour LC50
loses one %/h net.

| Reading | Alert edge | A mid-hardiness fish is harmed from | Breaks even | LC50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free NH₃ | 0.02 ppm | 0.028 ppm | ≈ 0.17 ppm | ≈ 1 ppm |
| Nitrite | 0.5 ppm | 0.71 ppm | ≈ 2.7 ppm | ≈ 10 ppm |
| Nitrate | 80 ppm | 113 ppm | ≈ 300 ppm | ≈ 800 ppm |
| Oxygen | 4 mg/L | 2.8 mg/L | ≈ 2.2 mg/L | ≈ 1.5 mg/L |

#### Predation

Every larger fish hunts a smaller one by the grams it outweighs it. Vulnerability
reads the prey's size against its own species' adult mass, so only the young are
hunted:

```
predator mass = Σ over every fish of max(0, its mass − prey mass)
hunted        = predationRate × predator mass / water × (1 − size / 100)^preyVulnerabilityExponent
```

hardened by `1 − hardiness`. A grown neon pair in 30 gal costs a newborn neon
0.45 %/h and a guppy pair a newborn guppy 0.35 %/h, about half what a fed fry
earns. A fed fry earning past it grows out of reach; a hungry or crowded one
dies — so a tank breeds until its own fish hold the fry back.

#### Ageing

```
wear    = wearAtLifespan × 2^((age − L) / (wearDoublingShare × L)) × species hardiness / its own
healing = healingDrawRate × mass^massScalingExponent × 2^(−age / (healingHalvingShare × L))
```

for species lifespan `L`. Species hardiness doesn't shield wear; only an
individual's offset does. Wear passes a fed fish's income a little before `L`,
then drains its bank and health over months; chronic stress brings that sooner.

### Individual variation

Sex, a hardiness offset of ±15 % of the species baseline and a small health
jitter are sampled once at construction. The offset staggers a cohort's deaths.
Stocked and bred fish come from the same factory.

### Growth and broods

A fish's size is its mass in % of its species' adult mass, and it splits the
bank: `size / 100` is its brood share, the rest its growth share. Each hour,
after vitality, clutches advance, females on full banks brood, then every bank
draws toward growth:

```
drawn    = bank × (1 − e^−growthDrawRate) × (1 − size / 100)
asked    = bodyOrganicShare × M × drawn × growthRate × growthPerSurplus / 100
supply   = digested × assimilatedFraction × growthEfficiency
retained = asked × supply / (supply + asked)
mass    += retained / bodyOrganicShare
bank    −= drawn × retained / asked
```

The asking is a share of the fish's metabolic mass `M` — anabolism runs on
mass to the ¾ like the rest of its metabolism — so growth is a specific rate on
mass to the −¼ that tapers logistically: on a full bank a hatchling grows
fastest, and a fish near adult size barely at all. Fed all it can eat, a
newborn reaches adult size in about two and a half months as a guppy, three as
a betta, four as a neon, five as a corydoras and seven as an angelfish. Supply scales with `M` too,
so a fry on a thin ration stunts at any size: growth never builds more
than its supply, and slows smoothly when digestion is short, the bank paying
only for what was built. What it retains leaves the gill stream, so a growing
fish excretes less ammonia per gram of food; a grown one retains none. Size
approaches 100 without a clamp. Fry and adult split at 50 %, where the brood
share passes the growth share.

A brood costs a parent `broodCost` bank points per its own body mass of
offspring:

```
eggs = brood share × bank × mass / (broodCost × egg mass)
```

A female lays the whole eggs her share buys, never more than her body can make
and still weigh something, and pays for those; the fathered ones are made of
her body, which loses what they hold. Each male of her
species fathers what his brood share pays for at his species' `maleShare` of the
price. Ready females of a species brood together: the males father up to all
eggs laid, shared back in proportion, each paying the same share of what he
could. Unfathered eggs are lost with what she paid. Refilling the banks is the
cooldown.

Stocking names a size, grown by default. Age starts at 0 and is read only by
wear and healing. Breeding is uncapped: the stocking ceiling gates only the
keeper's hand.

### Clutches

A clutch is a count of eggs, each weighing what a real egg of its species does
and holding its hatchling's body and a day's maintenance ration of yolk.
Every hour two competing first-order losses thin it and one rate develops it:

| Rate | Per hour |
|---|---|
| Harm | `eggSensitivity × Σ water stressors / 100` at the species' hardiness; a carried brood's at its mother's hardiness, without `eggSensitivity` |
| Predation | `eggPredationRate × exposure × predator mass / water`, every fish by the grams it outweighs an egg; none on a carried brood |
| Development | `metabolic factor / developmentTime` |

At development 1 each whole egg left hatches into a fry of the egg's mass with
its yolk in its gut; the part egg goes to waste. Eggs the water kills go to waste; eaten eggs go into the eaters' guts by
the grams each outweighs an egg, as room allows, the rest to waste.

| Species | Mode | Hatches in, at 25 °C | Exposure | Egg and fry | Male share | Grown brood |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Guppy | livebearer | 4 weeks, carried | 0 | 5 mg | 0.2 | 20 fry |
| Neon Tetra | egg-scatterer | 27 h | 1 | 0.4 mg | 0.2 | 125 eggs |
| Betta | bubble-nester | 36 h | 0.3 | 0.5 mg | 0.6 | 600 eggs |
| Corydoras | egg-depositor | 4 days | 1 | 4 mg | 0.2 | 100 eggs |
| Angelfish | substrate-spawner | 2½ days | 0.5 | 2 mg | 0.5 | 750 eggs |

Exposure is how much of the tank's predation reaches a laid clutch: a grown
neon pair in 30 gal finds an eighth of an open clutch an hour, so one egg in
twenty-five hatches, and every fry already swimming finds more. A
livebearer carries one brood at a time through gestation — a gestating female
doesn't brood — and it dies or leaves the tank with her.

### Death

Health reaching zero removes the fish. Its remains — its whole body, its gut
and any carried brood — rot to waste, less the share its
surviving predators eat: their share of its dying hour's damage, into their guts
by weight as room allows.
