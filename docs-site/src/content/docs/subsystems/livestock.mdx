## What it models

Every fish is an individual with a mass, a health, a gut and a bank. Food fills
the gut and the gut digests, and what it digests is the fish's income — the way
nutrient sufficiency is a plant's: every benefit is earned on it, and hunger
harms a fish digesting less than it needs. Its whole metabolism — digestion, the
oxygen it draws, the carbon it exhales — rides on one oxygen factor, so a
suffocating fish loads the water less and suffers more. Health is the vitality
model: income at full health banks, the bank heals the fish, and the bank buys
its growth and its broods, so a bigger ration grows a fish sooner and buys more
broods.

## How it works

### The roster

| Species | Adult mass | Growth rate | Lifespan | Hardiness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neon Tetra | 0.5 g | 0.5 | 5 y | 0.5 |
| Guppy | 1.0 g | 1 | 3 y | 0.8 |
| Betta | 3.0 g | 0.7 | 3 y | 0.6 |
| Corydoras | 4.0 g | 0.45 | 5 y | 0.7 |
| Angelfish | 15.0 g | 0.35 | 10 y | 0.4 |

| Species | Temperature | pH | GH | Max turnover |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neon Tetra | 22–28 °C | 5.0–7.8 | 1–12 dGH | 10 ×/h |
| Guppy | 22–28 °C | 6.5–8.5 | 6–25 dGH | 13 ×/h |
| Betta | 24–30 °C | 6.0–8.0 | 3–15 dGH | 5 ×/h |
| Corydoras | 22–26 °C | 6.0–8.0 | 2–15 dGH | 15 ×/h |
| Angelfish | 24–30 °C | 6.0–8.0 | 3–15 dGH | 10 ×/h |

pH and GH bands are the generous hobby care-sheet ranges, wide enough for fresh aqua soil and hard tap alike — soft-water species reach lower, the livebearer higher. Past an edge the damage grows with the distance, per pH unit or per dGH.

Flow tolerance is a turnover, not a flow, and a fixed-output device is what makes
the difference show: the smallest powerhead moves `908` L/h whatever it is
dropped into — a gentle `6` ×/h in a 150 L, and `45` ×/h in a 20 L, four and a
half times what a neon tolerates. A filter's flow scales with the tank instead,
so a canister lands on `8` ×/h in both.

### Metabolism

Mass drives every number, and one oxygen factor scales most of them.

| Flow | Per hour | Scaled by the oxygen factor |
|---|---|---|
| Digested | `gut × (1 − e^−k)`, `k = digestionRate × metabolic factor` | yes |
| Maintenance | `maintenanceRation × mass / 24 × metabolic factor` | yes |
| Eaten | the room left in the gut, `gutCapacity × mass − gut`, or its share of short food | no |
| Oxygen drawn | `baseRespirationRate × mass` | yes |
| CO₂ exhaled | the oxygen drawn × `respiratoryQuotient`, through the molar mass step | yes — it derives from the draw |
| Gill ammonia | digested × `foodNitrogenFraction` × `gillNFraction`, as NH₃ | through digestion |
| Feces | digested × `(1 − gillNFraction)` | through digestion |
| Minerals, beside the gill ammonia | digested × `gillNFraction` × `foodMineralContent` | through digestion |

The metabolic factor is the metabolic Q10 term times the oxygen factor, read
once an hour for the whole tank. What a fish digests and what it needs both
run on it, as a real ectotherm's maintenance falls with temperature: a cold
fish digests slower and needs less, and a fed gut holds it in cold or warm
water alike.

A fish regulates its uptake until the water falls past its critical oxygen
tension, so the half-saturation constant sits at 1.0 mg/L — the highest in the
tank. Fish feel a shortage before the bacteria do.

Every nitrogen and mineral flow starts from what the gut digests, so a meal
comes out over the hours after it is eaten and a fish with an empty gut
releases nothing. Food in the gut still carries its nitrogen and minerals, so
every milligram fed returns to the water — digested, through the gills or the
feces; uneaten, as it decays; in a fish that dies, with its body.

Damage is a separate reading. The oxygen stressor still charges a fish for the
water it is in, so a suffocating fish draws less *and* suffers more.

### Feeding and the gut

Every fish eats at once. Each wants the room left in its gut; while the food
lasts every appetite is met, and when it runs short each fish takes the same
share of its own appetite, so a school fills and thins together. Food the fish
leave stays in the water and decays, which is where overfeeding costs them.

The gut digests the same share of whatever it holds each hour, at
`digestionRate` at 25 °C times the metabolic factor — doubling for every ten
degrees warmer and slowing under oxygen the way the rest of the metabolism
does. A full gut is about half digested in seven hours and nearly clear in
thirty; a cold tank takes twice as long. A fish enters the tank with a day's
maintenance ration in its gut — a bought fish from the shop, a fry on its yolk.

What the gut digests this hour is the fish's income. Against its maintenance
ration — `maintenanceRation × mass` a day at 25 °C in ample oxygen, scaled by
the same metabolic factor, what holds its condition — it sets two rates:

| Rate | Shape | At maintenance |
|---|---|---|
| Nourishment | `digested / (digested + maintenance)`, scaling every benefit | half |
| Hunger | `hungerSeverity × (1 − digested / maintenance)` below it, hardened | nothing |

A ration past maintenance earns more of every benefit, so it banks faster,
grows sooner and buys more broods; one under it earns less and harms. Fed once
a day, a fish digests most of its meal in the first half of the day and runs a little hungry
before the next. Unfed, it stops banking at once and dies in about two weeks,
three with a full bank.

### Health

Health is the [vitality model](/concepts/vitality/): a fish heals out of its
income first, then out of its bank at a rate that falls with adult mass to the
−¼, and banks only what a full health leaves over. It is sick while its damage
outruns its healing, so its health falls.

| Stressor | Activates | Per |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | outside the species range | °C |
| pH | outside the species range | pH unit |
| GH | outside the species range | dGH |
| Free NH₃ | past the fish's own edge, of the *unionized* fraction read off pH and temperature | e-fold over |
| Nitrite | past the fish's own edge | e-fold over |
| Nitrate | past the fish's own edge | e-fold over |
| Oxygen | under the fish's own edge | e-fold under |
| Water level | below `waterLevelStressThreshold` of capacity | % under |
| Flow | above the species `maxTurnover` | tank volumes/h over |
| Hunger | digesting under its maintenance ration | share short of it |
| Age | past the species `maxAge` | hour over |

Every benefit is its peak times the fish's nourishment.

| Benefit | Earns when | Peak |
|---|---|---|
| pH | inside the species range, most at its centre and nothing at its edges | `phBenefitPeak` |
| Oxygen | rising on log scale from 4 mg/L, full from 6 | `oxygenBenefitPeak` |
| Plants | plant power, saturating at `plantBenefitSaturationPoint` | `plantBenefitPeak` |

At full nourishment pH and oxygen sum to 0.7 %/h in a clean tank with pH at the
band's centre, and a saturated planting lifts the budget to 0.9. That headroom
over zero is the whole point — a fed fish in a healthy tank sits at full health
with a positive net rate, banking what growth and broods spend.

#### Water quality

Each toxin stressor is its severity times the e-folds past the fish's own edge,
`ln(reading / edge)`, because toxicity runs on log dose: every doubling costs
the same. Oxygen counts e-folds under its edge, `ln((edge + a) / (reading + a))`,
with both sides read `a` = 0.1 mg/L higher so the harm stays finite at zero.
Hardiness moves a fish's edge out from the alert's by `2^hardiness`, and down by
the same factor for oxygen. Effective hardiness is clamped to [0.1, 0.95], so no
fish's edge reaches twice the alert's. A hardy species differs in where harm
starts, not in how steeply it grows, and still dies at the lethal levels. Each
severity puts a mid-hardiness fish at its 96-hour LC50 one %/h past what a clean
tank gives back, so an unbanked fish dies in about four days and a full bank
buys it about two more.

| Reading | Alert edge | A mid-hardiness fish is harmed from | Breaks even | LC50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Free NH₃ | 0.02 ppm | 0.028 ppm | ≈ 0.17 ppm | ≈ 1 ppm |
| Nitrite | 0.5 ppm | 0.71 ppm | ≈ 2.7 ppm | ≈ 10 ppm |
| Nitrate | 80 ppm | 113 ppm | ≈ 300 ppm | ≈ 800 ppm |
| Oxygen | 4 mg/L | 2.8 mg/L | ≈ 2.2 mg/L | ≈ 1.5 mg/L |

Ammonia reads the free fraction rather than total ammonia, so the same 2 ppm
reading costs a fish far less at pH 6.5 than at 8.0. Nitrite is read without
chloride, which in real tap water raises its tolerance.

### Individual variation

Sex, a hardiness offset of ±15 % of the species baseline, and a small health
jitter are sampled once when the fish is built and never re-rolled. Effective
hardiness is clamped to `[0.1, 0.95]`, so no offset makes a fish invincible or
instantly doomed.

The offset is what staggers deaths. Weaker individuals fail first as conditions
degrade, instead of a synchronised die-off. Stocked adults and bred fry come out
of the same factory, so a purchased fish and a tank-born one are
indistinguishable in shape.

### Growth and broods

A fish's size is its mass in % of its species' adult mass, and it splits the
bank: `size / 100` is its brood share, the rest its growth share. After
vitality settles, the females on full banks brood, then every fish's bank draws
toward growth.

```
drawn  = bank × (1 − e^−growthDrawRate) × (1 − size / 100)
size  += drawn × growthRate × sizePerSurplus
bank  −= drawn
```

A fry spends nearly all of its draw on mass and a fish near adult size almost
none, so size approaches 100 and never reaches it, and nothing clamps it. What
growth leaves banks, so a grown fish banks to the cap and a starved fry grows
slowly and breeds late. Fry and adult are readouts of size, split at 50 %,
where the brood share passes the growth share.

A full bank buys a female a brood with her brood share. A brood costs a parent
`broodCost` bank points per its own body mass of offspring, so her eggs are

```
eggs = brood share × bank × mass / (broodCost × fry mass)
```

— twenty guppy fry for a grown female on a full bank, seven for one at 60 %.
She lays the whole eggs that buys and pays for exactly those, so a female too
small to buy one keeps her bank. Each male of her species can father what his
own brood share pays for at his species' `maleShare` of that price. The ready
females of a species brood together in the hour: the males father as many as
they pay for between them, up to all the eggs laid, shared back to each female
in proportion to her eggs, and each male pays the same share of what his could.
Eggs nobody fathers are lost with what she paid for them. A male with an empty
bank fathers nothing, and each brood drains him, so one male cannot father
brood after brood. Refilling the banks is the cooldown — there are no timers.

| Species | Mode | Hatch | Fry mass | Male share | Grown brood |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Guppy | livebearer | direct | 5 % | 0.2 | 20 fry |
| Neon Tetra | egg-scatterer | 24 h | 5 % | 0.2 | 20 eggs |
| Betta | bubble-nester | 36 h | 3 % | 0.6 | 33 eggs |
| Corydoras | egg-depositor | 96 h | 4 % | 0.2 | 25 eggs |
| Angelfish | substrate-spawner | 60 h | 2 % | 0.5 | 50 eggs |

The male share is the father's investment: a bubble-nesting betta builds and
guards the nest, an angelfish pair fans its eggs together, a guppy or a neon
only mates. Livebearers drop free-swimming fry directly; every other mode lays
an inert clutch that hatches into fry at its hatch time, guarded by nobody and
eaten by nothing.

Stocking names a size, grown when none is named, from a fry's up to 100. Age
starts at 0 and is read only by old age.

Breeding is uncapped. The physical stocking ceiling gates the keeper's hand
only, so a bred cohort can push total mass past it, at which point further
stocking is refused while the population is left to its own dynamics.

### Death

Death is vitality-driven and nothing else: health reaching zero removes the fish
and adds `deathDecayFactor` of its body mass to the waste pool. Old age arrives
through the same channel — past the species lifespan the age stressor
accumulates until it outruns the benefit budget, which gives the keeper a
visible decline rather than a coin flip.

## Key tunables

| Constant | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `gutCapacity` | Food a full gut holds, per gram of fish | `0.03` g/g |
| `digestionRate` · `metabolicQ10` | First-order rate a gut digests at 25 °C, and how the metabolism — digestion and the maintenance ration — scales with temperature | `0.1` /hr · `2` |
| `maintenanceRation` | Food a day, per gram of fish, that holds condition at the reference temperature in ample oxygen — nourishment is half here, and hunger starts below it | `0.005` g/g/day |
| `hungerSeverity` | Damage at an empty gut, before hardiness | `0.6` %/hr |
| `baseRespirationRate` | Oxygen draw per gram, a Monod maximum | `0.3` mg O₂/g/hr |
| `respirationOxygenHalfSaturation` | O₂ at which the whole metabolism runs at half rate — digestion, draw and CO₂ alike | `1.0` mg/L |
| `foodNitrogenFraction` | Nitrogen in food, and in the waste it becomes | `0.05` g N/g |
| `gillNFraction` | Share of digested nitrogen leaving through the gills; the rest is feces | `0.8` |
| `respiratoryQuotient` | CO₂ exhaled per O₂ consumed, a molar ratio | `0.8` |
| `ammoniaStressSeverity` · `nitriteStressSeverity` · `nitrateStressSeverity` · `oxygenStressSeverity` | Damage per e-fold past the fish's own edge; oxygen's per e-fold under | `0.56` · `0.75` · `1.0` · `2.7` %/e-fold/hr |
| `flowStressSeverity` | Damage per turnover unit above species tolerance | `0.3` %/×/hr |
| `ghStressSeverity` | Damage per dGH outside the species range — a chronic harm, so a guppy five degrees short stays inside a fed tank's recovery | `0.1` %/dGH/hr |
| `ageStressSeverity` | Damage per hour past the species lifespan | `0.05` %/hr/hr |
| `phBenefitPeak` · `oxygenBenefitPeak` · `plantBenefitPeak` | The three benefit channels, at full nourishment | `0.4` · `0.3` · `0.2` %/hr |
| `plantBenefitSaturationPoint` | Plant power at which shelter tops out — three full healthy java fern clumps, or a little over one sword | `3.0` |
| `surplusCap` | Ceiling on the bank; a female broods on a full one | `50` condition points |
| `healingDrawRate` | First-order rate a 1 g fish's bank heals it at, scaled by adult mass to the −¼ | `0.05` /hr |
| `growthDrawRate` | First-order rate the bank draws toward growth at, before the growth share | `0.02` /hr |
| `sizePerSurplus` | Size, in % of adult mass, a bank point buys at growth rate 1 — a fed guppy grows out in about two and a half months, a neon in about five | `0.15` %/pt |
| `broodCost` | Bank points a brood of its parent's own weight costs the parent — the female in full, the male at his share | `50` pts |
| `deathDecayFactor` | Share of body mass that becomes waste on death | `0.5` |
