The engine has a face: a browser dashboard that drives one tank in real time,
deployed at [engine.fishroom.app](https://engine.fishroom.app). It is the
engine's showcase and its calibration instrument — the surface where a
month-long run becomes something you can watch, scrub back through, and explain.

It runs on the engine's API — build a tank, tick it, dispatch actions, read the
state back — and it does not stop there. It imports source directly rather than
the built package, and a handful of the numbers it displays come off thresholds
and formatters the package never exports. What it shows is therefore wider than
the published surface on its own.

## What it is

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Source | `src/ui/` |
| Stack | React, Vite, Tailwind, client-side routing |
| Relationship to the engine | A host with reach past the barrel. It calls `tick`, dispatches actions and reads state, and imports internals besides |
| Shipped in the package | No. The npm package is the engine; the dashboard deploys as a static site |
| Session state | The browser's local storage. There is no server and no account |
| Scope | One tank at a time |

## The six modules

| Module | Route | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Overview | `/` | The tank as a grid of widgets — the nitrogen cycle as a chain of stocks across the top-left, life beside it, water, gear and nutrients below — each a window onto its module. Anything the engine has latched an alert on is named above the grid, one line each, with the verb that answers it |
| Water | `/water` | The tank as a lab sheet, two columns of headed reading rows. Left: water (temperature, pH, level), the dissolved gases, and the four nutrients against what the plants ask for. Right: the nitrogen compounds on their alert lines, then the biofilter — each guild against its ceiling, what it converts an hour, whether the tank reads as cycled and where the nitrite peak falls — and the waste pool feeding it. Any row opens its reading in the drawer |
| Life | `/life` | Two tables on one row shape: the fish, and the plants with the algae riding along at their top as a population row. A species row carries a silhouette, the count, the figure each, a dot per individual and the group's condition, and opens onto the individuals under it; bioload sits under the fish against the stocking guideline. Under the fish rows sit the clutches counting down to hatch and one row for every fry in the tank, which is the unit the sell-all-fry verb takes. Any row opens the vitality ledger in the drawer — helping against hurting, each factor at its rate per day, the bank between them — and the module's "+" stocks a species through the same drawer |
| Gear | `/gear` | A rack of eight device rows: a power switch, a silhouette, the name, and what it is set to — or, for the three that keep a clock, that day as a 24 h ribbon against the hour the tank is on. A row opens the device's inspector in the drawer, addressed by the URL, so each inspector is a link and back walks between devices; closing one lands on the rack. Under the rack the scape — the substrate, and each piece of hardscape with the surface it adds — and under that what the fittings add up to, which is what the tick reads |
| History | `/history` | Four charts over the run — nitrogen, pH and CO₂, O₂ with temperature and level, populations — plus run tallies, the filtered event log, and a scrubber |
| Setup | `/setup` | What the tank *is*, rather than how it is doing: the preset it came from, its size, lid, room and tap water, and the figures those imply. Also the unit toggle and the run reset |

## The frame

Four bands that never move, whichever module is on the stage, and the one
drawer that lays over them.

| Element | Role |
|---|---|
| Top bar | The tank's preset on the left, the transport centred — play/pause, step, speed, the clock — and on the right the count of what needs the keeper, the Act palette, the tunables badge, theme, docs and source |
| Icon rail | The six modules, fixed, never scrolling, carrying no figures. A dot on an icon says that module has an engine alert standing against it |
| Stage | The module itself, with its own title and summary line |
| Spine | The run along the bottom: day ticks, alert and action marks, and the playhead. Dragging parks the playhead in the address, so the spine and the charts are never on different ticks and back walks out of a scrub; the handle opens History wherever it is parked |
| Drawer | The one inspector, laid over the right of the stage — no scrim, and the tank keeps ticking behind it. One at a time; Escape, a pointer outside it, or its own close button dismisses it |

Below the tablet breakpoint the rail folds into a five-tab bar with the last two
modules behind More, the transport keeps play and the clock, the drawer becomes
a full-height sheet from the bottom.

### Reading a value

Every reading in the dashboard is the same shape: name, value and unit, a range
strip, a trend, and — where the number alone does not say it — a note. The strip is a 4 px track with the engine's own
reference band lit on it and the value as a marker — inside the band the marker
and the number stay ink, outside they take the engine's severity. Fullness never
carries meaning, and a reading with no band shows an unlit track and stays
quiet.

Where the engine has no threshold of its own, the band is the tank's: the
temperature and pH spans every stocked species tolerates, plant demand for the
four nutrients. An unstocked tank has neither, and those readings go quiet.

Tapping a reading anywhere — the Overview or the Water module — opens the same
inspector in the drawer: the number at hero size, the band stated in the
engine's words, the last seven days for a reading the snapshot buffer records,
and — for the stocks of the nitrogen cycle — what fills them and what drains
them, at the rate the next tick will use. A flow arm moving less than the
precision its unit is read to prints as `none`, and only a stock's net — its
fills against its drains — ever prints as `steady`; neither prints a signed zero.

Bands are stated at the precision their reading is, so a value sitting on the
edge of its band reads as sitting on the edge. A tolerance band is hard on both
sides: the engine charges a temperature or pH stressor from the moment the
value leaves the span, and the strip tints on the same step.

## Time

The dashboard owns the clock; the engine only knows how to advance an hour.

| Control | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Play / pause | Autoplay ticks on an interval — one, six or twenty-four simulated hours per real second |
| Step | Advances a full simulated day, whatever the speed, and takes the clock so autoplay cannot carry you past the day you asked to look at |
| Space | Steps when paused, pauses when running |

Every tick is recorded as a compact vitals snapshot in a rolling buffer of the
last thirty days. That buffer is what the charts and the scrubber read, so
scrubbing never touches the simulation — the tank can keep running while you
look at last Tuesday.

## Acting on the tank

Every husbandry verb has a home: Feed, Trim and Scrub on Life, Water change and
Top-off on Water, Dose on Nutrients. Each appears as a button in that widget's
footer and in its module header, carrying the amount it is standing on — a
footer says what would happen rather than opening a menu to ask. The **Needs
you** strip carries the same verb beside the condition that calls for it.

The Act button in the top bar (and ⌘K) opens the palette: the six verbs with
their amounts and their modules, then the three construction verbs, filtered as
you type. Either route opens the identical sheet in the drawer.

| Part of the sheet | What it is |
|---|---|
| Amount rungs | The engine's own option sets — the water-change fractions it accepts, the trim targets, the feed and dose presets, plus the dose the plants are currently short of |
| Preview | The action applied to a throwaway state, read back as reading rows: the standing value as a ghost marker, the value the commit would leave as the live one, on the strip the reading is read on everywhere else. Only what moves is listed |
| Commit | One button, named for the verb and its amount — "Feed 0.5 g", "Change 25 % water" |
| Refusals | The engine's own guards, standing where the commit would be, so an unavailable verb is never a dead end |
| Dispatch | One call into the engine, which works whether the tank is running or paused, and writes the log line the spine marks |

The amount chosen for a verb outlives a dismissal: closing the sheet means "not
now", the footer buttons and the palette go on naming that amount, and the top
bar's Act button takes the name of the last verb committed.

Construction is not husbandry and does not go through this path. Swapping a
filter, resizing the tank, adding a plant or a rock edits the tank directly and
writes its own line into the log; the palette's construction verbs hand the
reader to the picker that owns them.

## Reading the engine

Nothing the dashboard displays is stored twice. Every reading, breakdown and
projection is derived from the state the last tick returned, so a reading
and the tank can never disagree. The Overview widgets and the Water module draw
their rows from one reading set, so the same reading cannot carry a different
band or a different word depending on which surface is showing it.

| Surface | Derived from |
|---|---|
| Reading values and status | Current resources, against the thresholds the engine alerts on |
| Reading bands | An engine threshold where there is one, the stocked species' tolerance or the plants' demand where there is not |
| The cycle chain | The engine's own mineralisation, oxidation and clearance rates, read for the hour ahead |
| Vitality breakdowns | The engine's own per-factor stressor and benefit lists, per organism |
| Charts | The snapshot buffer, each series normalised to its own extent so a compound at 0.1 ppm and one at 40 ppm share a frame without sharing a scale |
| Event log | The log entries the tick appends, filtered by category, exportable as text |
| Alert markers | Log entries of alert severity, placed on the charts and the scrubber at the tick they fired |
| Run tallies | Folded from the same log stream — deaths, births, alerts, water changed |

In History the tick cursor is the URL. A parked tick is a link, back walks out
of a scrub rather than out of the app, and a window that cannot honour the tick
resolves it and rewrites the address to match.

## The debug panel

The tunables drawer, behind the top bar's badge, exposes the engine's tunable constants,
grouped the way the config layer groups them, editable live. A changed value
takes effect on the next tick — no rebuild, no reload.

| Behaviour | Detail |
|---|---|
| Coverage | Ten of the eleven config sections. The livestock section and the nested fertilizer formula are not reachable from the panel |
| Validation | Each field is bounded by the range its tunable declares. Typing walks through values the range refuses, so a bad value holds the field and blur settles it rather than clamping mid-keystroke |
| Modified state | A changed value and its section are both marked, and a dot on the icon says the tank is no longer running on stock constants |
| Reset | Per section, or the whole config at once |

Tuned constants persist with the session, which means a tank reopened tomorrow
is still running the constants you left it on.

## Units

Every value the engine holds is metric. The unit system is a display choice,
converted at the edge and converted back on the way in, so switching it never
touches the tank.

| Quantity | Metric | Imperial |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | °C | °F |
| Volume | L | gal |
| Flow | L/h | GPH |

The toggle lives in Setup. The initial choice comes from the browser's
locale, and the tank-size picker offers round numbers in whichever system is
active — litres in one, gallons in the other.

## What persists

| Kept | Dropped |
|---|---|
| The tank: clock, resources, environment, equipment, plants, fish, clutches, algae, random stream, alert state | The event log, which starts fresh each session |
| The preset the tank came from | Transport speed and play state |
| Tuned constants | The History view — it lives in the URL instead |
| Units and whether the debug panel was open | |

Saves are validated section by section against a strict schema, so one corrupt
section falls back to its defaults rather than taking the rest with it. The
format carries a version, and a version it does not recognise is discarded
rather than migrated — the same no-backward-compatibility rule the engine holds
itself to.
